# Ports

Code: `src/panels/ports.rs`, `src/panels/ports/`, `src/panels/poll.rs`

- **The TUI's ports dialog as a sheet** (⇧⌘O, the rail's plug icon, or **Ports…** in a workspace's menu, which shows that workspace first): the TCP ports the workspace's programs listen on, from `ports --json`, with the URL, the addresses, the program and its arguments, its pid and how it belongs to the workspace (`pane 12 · dev`, `agent claude · pane 9`, `in the worktree, no tab`).
- **This workspace or every port**: the default is the workspace on screen; **Every port** passes `--all` and names each port's workspace, or "no workspace". Ports Docker publishes show only there, which the sheet says.
- **Refreshed every 2 s while open**, like Processes (`poll::Watch`); a change of the workspace on screen drops the old list.
- **Open shows the port in the Browser panel** (since 2026-10-31): the sheet closes, the dock shows the Browser, and the URL opens in the workspace on screen, or that workspace's tab already on the same origin comes forward (`Store::open_in_browser`, `design/browser.md`). On another machine, where the URL is that machine's `localhost`, the Browser tunnels it first (through cornercase's hold from 0.13.50, else its own ssh), so a remote port is one click too, as it is here. On this Mac the system browser stays, as the icon button beside Open; **Copy** stays everywhere.
  - Rejected: keeping the system browser as Open (the panel exists to show the page beside the agent changing it), only Copy and a note to run `ssh -L` by hand on another machine (what it offered until then), and opening a port of another workspace (under **Every port**) in its own workspace: the panel shows the tabs of the workspace on screen, so the tab would open out of sight.
- **On another server a row can be tunnelled through cornercase** (#106, from cornercase 0.13.50 on both computers; `design/browser.md` has the way and what happens when it is older): a port with a URL shows `Tunnel`, busy `Tunnelling…` while its hold comes up, then `Close tunnel` (busy `Closing…`), and a mark by its address: `⇄ :5174` (up, on that local port), `⇄ …` (opening), `⇄ down` (retrying, the reason as its tooltip) or `⇄ elsewhere` (held by another window or program as a forward tunnel: `cornercase tunnels` lists it, and Tunnel stays offered since it may be on another computer; a reverse hold never gives it, #196). Copy and the system-browser icon use `http://localhost:LOCAL` once this window's tunnel is up, so they work from this computer; Open shows the remote URL to the Browser, which maps it through the hold. The note above the list says so (`These ports are on devbox: Tunnel forwards one to this Mac through cornercase, which lists it, and Open shows it in the Browser panel.`); with an older cornercase on either side it keeps the old sentence and adds the one that names what 0.13.50 adds. What a row shows is a plain function of the way, this window's hold and what the view line lists (`store/tunnels/rows.rs`), so it is tested without a window.
  - Rejected: asking before Close tunnel (Tunnel undoes it, as upstream's menu does); tunnelling every listed port is rejected in `design/browser.md`. The system-browser icon sits right after Open, then Tunnel or Close tunnel.
- **A machine's workspace has its ports here** (#155): with a machine's place picked (⇧⌘O, or **Ports…** in its menu) the sheet lists that workspace's ports, read through `--remote` (`design/machines.md`, "A machine's ports and tunnels"). Its rows have Open (when Tunnel is offered; it shows the port in the Browser panel, `design/machines.md`, "A machine's page in the Browser panel"); Tunnel, Close tunnel, Copy and the system browser's icon work as on another server, once both cornercase releases allow it, and Stop names the machine.
- **Stop asks first** and sends `ports stop PORT --pid PID`; only a port of a workspace has it, as cornercase refuses the others. The confirmation says when an agent started the program and when no tab did (found by its folder). While it stops, Stop reads `Stopping…`, as in Processes (`poll::confirm_stop`, `design/processes.md`).
- **A tick per row and one button** (`ports::to_stop`, the TUI's bottom button, 0.12.204): the toolbar's button says `Stop 2…` for the ticked ports, or with none ticked `Stop all…` for every port of the workspace; only a port that Stop would take has a tick. Under **Every port** it needs ticks: `Stop all` there would reach every workspace's servers at once, and the TUI's button is about one workspace too. A tick lasts while its port is listed.
- **One question for all of them**: it names each program, port and pid and warns when an agent or no tab started some, and it holds what was listed when it was asked, so a refresh cannot change what stops. One `ports stop PORT --pid PID` per port, side by side and beside the queue, since each may wait 3 s; each row shows `Stopping…` on its own and a failure toasts on its own (`poll::confirm_stops`). One port asks the question Stop asks.
- Ticks and Stop all checked live on 2026-11-06 (debug build, cornercase 0.13.1, Linux x86_64 under Xvfb with lavapipe, a private server; no macOS, no Windows): two `python3 -m http.server` in panes, a tick and its button, Stop all, both programs ended and their shells still there. Ticks under **Every port** were not tried.
- Checked live on 2026-10-29 with cornercase 0.12.225 (a `python3 -m http.server` in a throwaway worktree). Open into the Browser panel checked on 2026-10-31 with cornercase 0.12.234, on this Mac and through `--remote` to a throwaway sshd on 127.0.0.1 (`design/browser.md`).
