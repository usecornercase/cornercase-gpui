# Queue

Code: `src/panels/queue.rs`, `src/panels/queue/`

- **The TUI's Queue tab, for every project**: the entries come live from `status --app` (its `queue`, the same as `queue list --json`'s `entries`). The panel never polls `queue list`; the app stream already carries every change.
- **Entries are read leniently** (`#[serde(default)]`, unknown fields ignored, an entry without an id dropped): a field a newer server adds or drops never hides an entry.
- **States** are the server's: `pending`, `starting`, `started`, `failed`, `missed`. Pending, failed and missed entries can be edited and started now (`runnable`); a starting one cannot be removed until it settles.
- **An empty queue explains itself** (asked for on 2026-10-31: with nothing in it, the sheet did not say what it was for, only a grey line under an empty filter). Until the queue holds an entry, the sheet is a centred page in the look of Issues' page for a tracker that is not connected (`ui::emblem`, shared with it): the Queue's emblem, "Start agents later, on their own", a line saying that nothing is queued yet and what the queue does, **Schedule an agent…** (the form) beside **Open Issues**, whose Schedule… queues an issue, and a card of four points. The buttons come before the card, so a short window shows them without scrolling. The points are what the form asks, in its order, with its icons: when (a time, a delay, a plan's reset), what (a prompt or an issue), where (a new tab or a new worktree) and that it runs unattended. The toolbar is left out, since there is nothing to filter. While the form is open the page stays without its buttons, so the points explain the form beside them. The card of points sits in a full-width row that centres it, and grows to the row's width up to 520 (`flex_1`, `min_w(0)`, `max_w(520)`): in the page's column, taffy 0.9 measures an item's height at its width before `max_w` caps it, so a card given the page's width, or 520 capped by the page, ran its last point out of it; given no width, it took its texts' one-line width (gpui measures a text's narrowest width as its whole line) and spilled out of a narrow page. Along a row's main axis the cap applies first, so its height fits (all three seen live on 2026-10-31, at 1280 and 900 px wide).
- **Sections, then the server's order**: "Did not start" (failed and missed, which wait for Start now or another time), "Scheduled" (pending and starting) and "Started", each in the server's order (chronological, ties by id). In the one chronological list the TUI draws, what started earlier pushed what starts next down the sheet, and a failed entry sat among the started ones. A state a newer server adds goes under "Other" rather than under a heading that would misname it. The headings carry the counts, which replaced "N pending · M in all" in the toolbar, and Clear started sits in the Started heading, beside what it clears. Without a filter "Scheduled" always shows, and says when nothing is scheduled and how to schedule; a filter leaves out the sections it empties.
- **A row** is when it runs, the state's icon, the label (dimmer once started), the prompt's first line when it differs, and the state in its colour with agent, mode, place and accounts. The icons are the window's status family (`design/widgets.md`): pending a dot in a ring, a spinner while starting, a green check once started, a red cross for failed, the orange "!" for missed, since it waits for you. The TUI colours missed yellow, which reads as "working" here. A failed entry says why, and a missed one what happened and what to do, on a line of their colour under the row.
- **Actions show on the row under the pointer**, as Processes' Go and Stop and the TUI's buttons, which follow the selected row: otherwise Start now, Edit and Remove repeated on every row, a cobalt Start now on each. They stay on the row being edited, and `Starting…` stays while `queue run` answers. They are hidden (`invisible`), not transparent, so nothing hidden takes a click. A click on the row does what Enter does in the TUI: it edits an entry that can run and shows a started one's live pane (`data::Click`); it does nothing while the entry starts or is already in the form. Remove's tooltip on a started entry says that its agent keeps running, which a line under the list used to say.
- **The toolbar** is the filter, a search field with its clear button as in Issues and Processes, and **Schedule…**.
- **The form** says what happens under its title ("It starts by itself at the time you choose"; an edit, that only what changes is sent), and its sections take the empty page's icons. The lists that wrap (trackers, projects, workspaces, agents, modes) are chips, as in Issues' start form; the short fixed choices stay segments. Its fields wrap (`TextField::wraps`), so a long prompt or issue URL grows the field instead of running out of it. A footer stays while the form scrolls: the trust notice, the error with its icon, Cancel and Schedule (Save for an edit).
- **"Started · pane N" only while that pane exists**: an entry records the server token (`boot` in `status --app`) its pane belongs to. A pane id from another server run, or one the session no longer has, reads "pane closed" and offers no Open button, since pane ids are reused across restarts.
- **Places read as the TUI names them**: `project › branch`, else `project › workspace name`, else the worktree folder; a project the session no longer has falls back to its folder name.
- **Local time comes from `localtime_r`** (libc), so the panel needs no time-zone crate and agrees with the machine's zone. The `unsafe` is sound because `localtime_r` writes only the zeroed `tm` it is given, on the caller's stack. Day differences use Howard Hinnant's days-from-civil algorithm (`time::days`), which the search panel reuses for RFC 3339 times.
- **When a row runs reads from today** (`today 14:30`, `tomorrow`, `yesterday`, a weekday within the week, the date otherwise) plus a relative time (`in 2h 5m`, `3d 4h ago`). A 30-second timer redraws the panel so relative times move on by themselves.
- **The form builds `queue add` or `queue edit ID`** with `--json`, so the answer can be read and an error shown under the form (with the `cornercase queue: ` prefix removed). `--at` takes the texts the CLI documents (`14:30`, `tomorrow 9:00`, `2026-10-24 09:00`, `in 2h`, `claude reset`, `codex reset`). The entry starts from `--issue` (a URL or key) or `--prompt`; an issue may name its tracker (`--source`) for an ambiguous key or a self-hosted URL.
- **An edit sends only what changed**: the time stays unless another kind is chosen ("unchanged"), the place stays unless "somewhere else" is chosen, the agent stays unless one is picked, and the prompt and name are sent only when their text differs from the entry's.
- **A new entry starts where the window is**: the shown project and workspace, filled in once the session says where that is (it may arrive after the form opens). Choosing a project without a workspace means a new worktree on a branch, made when missing.
- **Agents and modes come from `config --json`** (setting `agent` and `agents.NAME.mode`), read once when the form first opens; `auto` has no modes. The mode chosen in the settings is marked with ✓.
- **An entry that started or went away meanwhile closes its edit form**, since the server would refuse the edit.
- **Removing asks nothing and can be undone** (#463 upstream, checked with cornercase 0.12.225): `queue remove ID --json` and `queue clear-started --json` answer the removed entries and an `undo` number, and the panel offers Undo (`queue restore N`) for 6 seconds in an undo bar, as the TODO panel and the TUI's undo toast do. A later removal replaces the bar; the timer only clears the bar it was started for. The server keeps only its last few removals, so an old number can be refused, which becomes a toast. Removing a started entry only clears history: its agent keeps running.
- **The undo bar sits under the toolbar**, outside the list's scroll, and over the empty page too: removing the last entry empties the queue, and its Undo must stay in reach.
- **Why a bar and not a toast with an action**: a toast's action runs through `Store::act`, which adds `--view`, and `queue restore` refuses `--view`.
- **Queued agents accept the folder's trust prompt and submit the prompt by themselves**: that is cornercase's behaviour for unattended starts, and the form says it.
- **`open_pane` and `toast_outcome` live here and are shared** with the search and TODO panels (focus a pane and close the sheet; toast a command's result), so the three panels do not repeat them.
- **Start now** reads `Starting…` until `queue run` answers (it starts an agent, maybe in a new worktree), and the status bar names the entry; Save reads `Saving…` and Schedule `Scheduling…`.
- **Checked live** on 2026-10-31 with cornercase 0.12.230 (debug build, 1280×800 and 900×640) on a throwaway project: the empty page, with the form open beside it and opened from it, a long prompt growing its field, the form's error, Open Issues; and, through a `CORNERCASE_BIN` wrapper that answers `status --app` with made-up entries, the sections, a row in every state, the actions on hover, a click opening a missed entry's form, a notice wrapping in the narrowed list, and a queue holding only started entries. Start now, Remove, Undo and Open were not pressed on the made-up entries, and nothing was scheduled (`queue list` stayed empty).
