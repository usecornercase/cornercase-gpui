# Queue

Code: `src/panels/queue.rs`, `src/panels/queue/`

- **The TUI's Queue tab, for every project**: the entries come live from `status --app` (its `queue`, the same as `queue list --json`'s `entries`). The panel never polls `queue list`; the app stream already carries every change.
- **Entries are read leniently** (`#[serde(default)]`, unknown fields ignored, an entry without an id dropped): a field a newer server adds or drops never hides an entry.
- **States** are the server's: `pending`, `starting`, `started`, `failed`, `missed`, and on a routine's runs `removed` and `kept`. Pending, failed and missed entries can be edited and started now (`runnable`), a routine's runs never; a starting one cannot be removed until it settles.
- **An empty queue explains itself** (asked for on 2026-10-31: with nothing in it, the sheet did not say what it was for, only a grey line under an empty filter). Until the queue holds an entry, the sheet is a centred page in the look of Issues' page for a tracker that is not connected (`ui::emblem`, shared with it): the Queue's emblem, "Start agents later, on their own", a line saying that nothing is queued yet and what the queue does, **Schedule an agent…** (the form) beside **Open Issues**, whose Schedule… queues an issue, and a card of five points. The buttons come before the card, so a short window shows them without scrolling. The points are what the form asks, in its order, with its icons: when (a time, a delay, a plan's reset), again and again (a routine, and its limits), what (a prompt or an issue), where (a new tab or a new worktree) and that it runs unattended. The toolbar is left out, since there is nothing to filter. While the form is open the page stays without its buttons, so the points explain the form beside them. The card of points sits in a full-width row that centres it, and grows to the row's width up to 520 (`flex_1`, `min_w(0)`, `max_w(520)`): in the page's column, taffy 0.9 measures an item's height at its width before `max_w` caps it, so a card given the page's width, or 520 capped by the page, ran its last point out of it; given no width, it took its texts' one-line width (gpui measures a text's narrowest width as its whole line) and spilled out of a narrow page. Along a row's main axis the cap applies first, so its height fits (all three seen live on 2026-10-31, at 1280 and 900 px wide).
- **Sections, then the server's order**: "Did not start" (failed and missed, which wait for Start now or another time), "Scheduled" (pending and starting), "Routines" and "Started", each in the server's order (chronological, ties by id). In the one chronological list the TUI draws, what started earlier pushed what starts next down the sheet, and a failed entry sat among the started ones. A state a newer server adds goes under "Other" rather than under a heading that would misname it. The headings carry the counts, which replaced "N pending · M in all" in the toolbar, and Clear started sits in the Started heading, beside what it clears. Without a filter "Scheduled" shows even when empty, and says when nothing is scheduled and how to schedule, unless a routine is listed, since then something is; a filter leaves out the sections it empties.
- **A row** is when it runs, the state's icon, the label (dimmer once started), the prompt's first line when it differs, and the state in its colour with agent, mode, place and accounts. The icons are the window's status family (`design/widgets.md`): pending a dot in a ring, a spinner while starting, a green check once started, a red cross for failed, the orange "!" for missed, since it waits for you. The TUI colours missed yellow, which reads as "working" here. A failed entry says why, and a missed one what happened and what to do, on a line of their colour under the row.
- **Actions show on the row under the pointer**, as Processes' Go and Stop and the TUI's buttons, which follow the selected row: otherwise Start now, Edit and Remove repeated on every row, a cobalt Start now on each. They stay on the row being edited, and `Starting…` stays while `queue run` answers. They are hidden (`invisible`), not transparent, so nothing hidden takes a click, and they keep their room while hidden, so a row does not change when the pointer reaches it. Edit keeps its slot while its row is in the form. A click on the row does what Enter does in the TUI: it edits an entry that can run, shows a started one's live pane and, on cornercase 0.13.51 or newer, opens the detail of one whose pane is gone (`data::Click`); it does nothing while the entry starts or is already in the form. Remove's tooltip on a started entry says that its agent keeps running, which a line under the list used to say.
- **The toolbar** is the filter, a search field with its clear button as in Issues and Processes, and **Schedule…**.
- **The form** says what happens under its title ("It starts by itself at the time you choose"; an edit, that only what changes is sent), and its sections take the empty page's icons. The lists that wrap (trackers, projects, workspaces, agents, modes) are chips, as in Issues' start form; the short fixed choices stay segments. The prompt is `TextField::prompt()` since 2026-11-09 (`design/widgets.md`), so a stray Enter no longer schedules half a prompt (#87); until then it was one line that wrapped. Schedule or Save, or ⌘Enter, sends it. The other fields (the issue's URL or key, time, date, amount, branch, name) stay one line that wraps, so a long URL grows the field, and Enter in them saves. An entry's prompt opened for an edit keeps its lines and is still sent only when it differs. The body is the form's scroller. `--prompt=TEXT` goes joined to its flag, as `flow run`'s are (`design/flows.md`, Start). The TUI's own schedule prompt is one line, so this window departs from it on purpose; both send a `--prompt` with lines, which cornercase keeps. A footer stays while the form scrolls: the trust notice, the error with its icon, Cancel and Schedule (Save for an edit).
- **"Started · pane N" only while that pane exists**: an entry records the server token (`boot` in `status --app`) its pane belongs to. A pane id from another server run, or one the session no longer has, reads "pane closed" and offers no Open button, since pane ids are reused across restarts. With 0.13.51 or newer that row opens its detail (see Results).
- **Places read as the TUI names them**: `project › branch`, else `project › workspace name`, else the worktree folder; a project the session no longer has falls back to its folder name.
- **Local time comes from `localtime_r`** (libc), so the panel needs no time-zone crate and agrees with the machine's zone. The `unsafe` is sound because `localtime_r` writes only the zeroed `tm` it is given, on the caller's stack. Day differences use Howard Hinnant's days-from-civil algorithm (`time::days`), which the search panel reuses for RFC 3339 times.
- **When a row runs reads from today** (`today 14:30`, `tomorrow`, `yesterday`, a weekday within the week, the date otherwise) plus a relative time (`in 2h 5m`, `3d 4h ago`). A 30-second timer redraws the panel so relative times move on by themselves.
- **The form builds `queue add` or `queue edit ID`** with `--json`, so the answer can be read and an error shown under the form (`cli::reason`, without the command's name or `Error: `). `--at` takes the texts the CLI documents (`14:30`, `tomorrow 9:00`, `2026-10-24 09:00`, `in 2h`, `claude reset`, `codex reset`). The entry starts from `--issue` (a URL or key) or `--prompt`; an issue may name its tracker (`--source`) for an ambiguous key or a self-hosted URL.
- **An edit sends only what changed**: the time stays unless another kind is chosen ("unchanged"), the place stays unless "somewhere else" is chosen, the agent stays unless one is picked, and the prompt and name are sent only when their text differs from the entry's.
- **A new entry starts where the window is**: the shown project and workspace, filled in once the session says where that is (it may arrive after the form opens). Choosing a project without a workspace means a new worktree on a branch, made when missing.
- **Agents and modes come from `config --json`** (setting `agent` and `agents.NAME.mode`), read once when the form first opens; `auto` has no modes. The mode chosen in the settings is marked with ✓.
  - **The Mode.** "as in settings" comes first and is lit on a new entry. It sends no `--mode`, so the server captures the mode settings chose, before 0.13.28 and after; upstream's form opens on that mode for the same reason, so an entry nobody changes schedules as before. Each choice of `agents.NAME.mode` follows.
  - **`default`** sends `--mode default`, which means no mode arguments from 0.13.28. Before, the server read it as the chosen mode, so on an older server it rests while another mode is chosen, with the issue start's line (`data::mode_chips`, `design/issues.md`). The queue runs on the window's server, so the release is the `view` line's.
  - **Each chip has an id of its own** (`q-mode-settings`, `("q-mode", index)`). With `q-mode-{mode}`, `default`'s chip had the same id as "as in settings", and in gpui 0.2.2 a global element id is the stack of ids, so both would share one pending press and the first one's capture phase would drop it. Read in gpui 0.2.2's code, not checked live: no build of the old code was tried. The Queue's form was not opened live on 0.13.28 or 0.13.21 either (checked as plain functions: `mode_chips`, built from `Agents` as the issue start's Mode is); the Start form's rule it shares was seen on both (`design/issues.md`).
- **An Account row follows the agent** (`design/accounts.md`, An account where agents start): a dropdown under Agent, for the chip chosen, else the entry's agent on an edit, else settings' (none for `auto`, which `queue add` refuses). Hidden when a routine runs a flow, as upstream's is: a team has several agents and the form asks for one. `--account` goes only when the dropdown differs from what the entry would get anyway, so an edit sends only what changed, as the rest of the form does. A new entry left alone follows the account in use when it starts, and a dim line says so. An entry's account that is gone reads `home (gone)` in red and saving it unchanged is refused by `queue edit`, in the form's error; a pick clears that error, as any change does. A row's `claude: work` names the folder, not the label: `status --app` gives the folder, and the row has no list to look it up in.
- **An entry that started or went away meanwhile closes its edit form**, since the server would refuse the edit.
- **Removing asks nothing and can be undone** (#463 upstream, checked with cornercase 0.12.225): `queue remove ID --json` and `queue clear-started --json` answer the removed entries (a routine's with its runs, which the bar says) and an `undo` number, and the panel offers Undo (`queue restore N`) for 6 seconds in an undo bar, as the TODO panel and the TUI's undo toast do. A later removal replaces the bar; the timer only clears the bar it was started for. The server keeps only its last few removals, so an old number can be refused, which becomes a toast. Removing a started entry only clears history: its agent keeps running.
- **The undo bar sits under the toolbar**, outside the list's scroll, and over the empty page too: removing the last entry empties the queue, and its Undo must stay in reach.
- **Why a bar and not a toast with an action**: a toast's action runs through `Store::act`, which adds `--view`, and `queue restore` refuses `--view`.
- **Queued agents accept the folder's trust prompt and submit the prompt by themselves**: that is cornercase's behaviour for unattended starts, and the form says it.
- **`open_pane` and `toast_outcome` live here and are shared** with the search and TODO panels (focus a pane and close the sheet; toast a command's result), so the three panels do not repeat them.
- **Start now** reads `Starting…` until `queue run` answers (it starts an agent, maybe in a new worktree), and the status bar names the entry; Save reads `Saving…` and Schedule `Scheduling…`.
- **Weeks in a delay**: `in 1w` is new in cornercase 0.13.1, so `in` shows the same four units as `every`.
- **Checked live** on 2026-10-31 with cornercase 0.12.230 (debug build, 1280×800 and 900×640) on a throwaway project: the empty page, with the form open beside it and opened from it, a long prompt growing its field, the form's error, Open Issues; and, through a `CORNERCASE_BIN` wrapper that answers `status --app` with made-up entries, the sections, a row in every state, the actions on hover, a click opening a missed entry's form, a notice wrapping in the narrowed list, and a queue holding only started entries. Start now, Remove, Undo and Open were not pressed on the made-up entries, and nothing was scheduled (`queue list` stayed empty).

## Routines

A routine is a queue entry that repeats (cornercase 0.13.1, #653 upstream, its `design/queue.md`): `repeat` is set, its state stays `pending`, and each time it fires a run is added, an ordinary entry whose `routine` names it. Its `next` is its next time; a paused routine has none, and its `at` is then stale, so it reads `paused` and `at` is never shown.

- **A section of its own, each run under its routine's label.** The TUI draws them in one chronological list, where a routine's runs sit among everything else; among "Scheduled" here a routine would swap places with one-off entries each time it fires, its history between them. The section says plainly, under its heading, that a routine only runs while cornercase's server runs, that each run gets a fresh worktree from the up-to-date default branch and that only git repositories can have routines, as the form does for a repeating kind and the empty page's card.
- **A run is one line under the label, in the label's column**: its icon, its time, its state, where it ran, and on a line of its state's colour under it what the TUI appends: why it failed, why it was kept, what a missed run means (no "Start it now", which is not on offer). `removed` and `kept` read "Workspace removed" and "Workspace kept", since "Removed" under a list with a Remove button says the wrong thing. Kept is the orange "!", since something waits for you there; removed a grey check.
- **Runs show newest first, at most 10.** The server keeps 10, and up to 20 while a run's cleanup counts down; the newest are the ones to see. A run whose routine is gone stays in its state's section, read as history. A filter that matches only a run keeps its routine above it.
- **A run is history**: no Edit, no Start now, a click only opens a live pane (a kept run has one too). Remove takes it from the list and leaves its agent and workspace, as the tooltip says. Clear started clears them too, never a routine; it sits in the Started heading, or in the Routines heading when only runs have something to clear (`clear_goes_in`), the same rule as the server's `clearable`.
- **Actions**: Start now, Pause or Resume, Edit, Remove, shown as the row's others are. Pause and Resume are icons with tooltips, as Edit and Remove are, so swapping one for the other moves nothing and a list beside the open form keeps room for its text; the reserved width of two more labelled buttons took it from the label. A spinner takes the icon's slot while `queue pause` or `queue resume` answers. Resume's icon is the repeat arrows rather than Start now's play. A routine cannot be removed while a run of it starts (the server refuses it), so Remove waits as it does on a starting entry.
- **A narrow list keeps its text.** With the form open in a 900 px window the list is about 320 px wide, and the room the hidden actions keep took all of the label's, four buttons now on a routine. A row wraps its actions to a line of their own rather than squeeze its text under 100 px (`flex_wrap`, with `ml_auto` to keep them on the right), and a run's indent gives way before its text does: it is a spacer beside the row, which has a minimum width, so the spacer shrinks. Seen live at 900×640.
- **In words**: `repeat.rs` reads the server's canonical texts (`2h`, `3d 01:25`, `day 09:00`, `weekday 09:00`, `monday 09:00`) into `↻ every 2 hours`, `every 3 days at 01:25`, `every Monday at 09:00`. A text it does not know reads `every <text>`, so a newer server's kind still says something true. A routine of days or weeks shows its clock, which the server captured when it was added.
- **The form offers one family on an edit**: the server refuses to turn a one-off entry into a routine or back, so a one-off's edit lists `unchanged` and the one-off kinds, and a routine's `unchanged` and the four repeating kinds (`When::offered`). `unchanged` on a routine shows its repeat in words, and the other kinds start from its values. `--every` goes only when another kind is chosen. A new entry lists both families, in two tracks, one above the other, their segments a little tighter than the form's others so that each fits a row at the form's default width. Widening the form to 460 instead left the list about 280 px in a 900 px window, too little for its toolbar.
- **The values go as typed, and the server's error is what shows**: below 30 minutes, above a year or a clock it cannot read are refused by `Repeat::parse` with its own words, in the form's error, so the limits live in one place. The form only asks for a value that is empty. A hint under `every` gives the limits beforehand.
- **A repeating kind changes the form**: What is the prompt alone (`--every` refuses an issue), and for a flow a line says it is the team's task. Where lists only repository roots and `Open in` says `new worktree`, with no branch and no choice, since a routine names its own worktrees (`--project` only). If the project in the form is not a repository, the form moves to the first that is, or says to open one (`repository_for`). Agent gains each flow as `<name> (flow)` (`--flow`; then Mode says it is set by the flow's roles), from `flow list --json` for the form's project, read once per project while a repeating kind is chosen: only those that can start, each name once, since a project's flow can share a name with yours.
- **A repository root is read from the session**: a workspace at the project's folder with a branch (a detached one reports its short hash, a plain folder none), or a linked worktree in the project. This panel does not read the disk (`AGENTS.md`). A project opened on a linked worktree passes here, though its `.git` is a file, and the server's refusal shows.
- **`remove the workspace when it finishes`** is off by default, sent as `--cleanup` or `--no-cleanup` on a new routine and on an edit only when changed. A line beside it says what it does and when the workspace stays, in the docs' terms. The TUI greys it for an agent whose status it cannot read. `config --json` does not say which those are (today they happen to be the ones without an `agents.NAME.mode` setting, which is no contract; #704 upstream), so here the box stays and the server's refusal shows, in the form's error, and a muted line under the box says so and names the issue.
- **Checked live** on 2026-11-06 with cornercase 0.13.1, Linux x86_64 under Xvfb with lavapipe, on a bench server with a real bare remote and a fake agent script (no real agent ran; no macOS, no Windows): through the window: a routine of each kind from the form (`30m`, `3d`, `2w`, `day 09:00` with the checkbox, `weekday 08:15`, `friday 18:00` on the flow Quick, each read back with `queue list --json`), every 10 minutes and the agent `auto` refused with the server's words in the form, a plain folder left out of Where and the form moving to the repository; Pause and Resume, Start now with a run appearing under its routine (a real fetch from a bare remote), a flow routine's run with fake roles from the command line, Remove taking a routine and its run with Undo bringing both back, Clear started taking three finished runs and no routine, an edit from `every 30 minutes` to `every day at 07:45` with the checkbox, an edit from one flow to another and from a flow to an agent, a one-off's edit offering no repeating kind, a click on a run opening its pane, Remove on one run, the empty page, and 900×640 beside the form. Real `removed`, `kept` and `failed` runs were made on the same bench: the fake agent set Claude's working and idle titles, so its status went `done`, and after the 3 minutes one clean run was removed and one with an uncommitted file kept; a remote that could not be fetched failed one run (`tests/fixtures/queue/routines.json`, with the paths replaced). The server's refusal of `--cleanup` for an agent it cannot read shows in the form too (`amp`). Not seen live: a `missed` run (unit tests only, it needs a server down past an occurrence) and the cleanup of a flow's run.

## A bot's entries

A bot's routines and reminders are queue entries (cornercase 0.13.85, usecornercase/cornercase-terminal#846): `place.bot` names the bot, `project` is empty, a run copies the place, and a run that answered is `answered`. **They are made, edited and listed in the bot's own sheet, never in the projects' queue** (part 2 of #267, following upstream's #888, kept by #896); how they show in the chat is in `design/bots.md`, Routines.

- **The projects' queue lists no bot entry** (`data::scoped` with no bot keeps the entries without `place.bot`), in any section, the filter's matches included. Upstream moved them out of its queue modal into the bot (#888) for two reasons this window shares: one place per bot for what it does on a schedule (the chat's Routines, beside its runs' blocks), and no list that mixes agents started in workspaces with a bot's answers, which have no pane, no worktree and no result to open. Rejected: keeping them under a heading of their own (a second place to read and act on the same entries, and the projects' sheet would still need the bot's form and words), and a filter chip (the same, hidden).
- **A dim line says where they are**, as upstream's tail: `2 bot routines: in each bot's chat, under Routines` (`1 bot routine: in its bot's chat, under Routines`), after the list and on the empty page under its buttons (`bot::elsewhere`). It counts routines and reminders, not runs, of bots in the list, as upstream does: an entry of a bot that is gone shows nowhere (upstream's rule), and its routines went with it anyway. `under Routines` is this window's addition, since the bot's view here is reached by that name.
- **The bot's sheet is where a bot's routine is made and edited**: Schedule… in its toolbar (`Schedule a routine or a reminder for PRs, without asking it`) and **Schedule a routine…** on its empty page open the Queue's form with the bot fixed (`Form::bot`, from the sheet's scope); Edit (the pencil) on a bot's entry that can run opens it with its values (`Form::bot` from the entry's `place.bot`). Rejected: a form of the bot's own in the chat (a second form for the same `queue add`), and the projects' Schedule… with a bot picked in Where (upstream's form fixes the bot, since the bot's dialog is where it opens from).
- **The form for a bot asks what a bot's entry takes**: What is the prompt alone (`--every` and a bot both refuse an issue), with `A run does not see the chat: it starts from the bot's memory and this.`; When as for a project (both families on a new entry, one on an edit); Where is text, `PRs's chat`, with what a run is (a new conversation of the bot, while cornercase's server runs); Agent is text, `Set by the bot: …` (upstream's `set by the bot`); Name. No Issue, projects, workspaces, branch, agent chips, account, mode, flows, cleanup box or trust note: nothing is trusted or submitted to a pane. The header reads `Schedule a routine` or `Schedule a reminder`, then `PRs answers in its chat …`. `config --json` and `flow list` are not read for it.
- **What it sends**: `queue add --every … | --at …, --prompt=…, --bot ID [--name …] --json`, and an edit `queue edit ID` with only what changed and never `--bot` (the old entry names it; the server refuses moving an entry between a bot and a project). `Draft::args` skips the place and the choices whole for a bot (`push_bot`), so nothing the server refuses on a bot's entry (`refuse_bot_mix`: project, workspace, worktree, issue, source, flow, cleanup, agent, mode, account) can be sent whatever the form's other fields hold. An empty prompt says `write what it should do`. The toast says `Routine scheduled`, `Reminder scheduled`, `Routine saved` or `Reminder saved`.
- **A new bot form starts on every day at 09:00** (`form::start`), as upstream's `+ routine` does: what a person asks a bot most is a daily summary, and with the time already chosen, typing the prompt and ⌘Return is the whole act with keys. Choosing a one-off kind makes it a reminder.
- **The gate has two halves.** The server's (`Feature::BotRoutines`, 0.13.85) decides whether the bot's sheet is reached at all (`design/bots.md`). This computer's: the command line checks the feature only when `--bot` is sent (upstream's `cli/queue.rs`, `needs_bot_routines` reads `add.bot` alone), so `queue add --bot` needs 0.13.85 here too and `queue edit` of a bot's entry does not. With an older command line here the bot's sheet offers no Schedule… and says `Scheduling for a bot from this window needs cornercase 0.13.85 or newer on this Mac (it has …).` above its list or on its empty page (`bot::add_note`); Edit, Start now, Pause, Resume and Remove stay. Not tried with an older command line: read in upstream's sources.
- **No detail on a bot's entry** (`offers_detail`): its answer is in the chat, not in `queue show`'s result. Its row's click goes to the run's block or does nothing (`bot_click`), never to the form, even on a pending reminder: a click that edits before the first run and opens the chat after it would change under the pointer; Edit is the pencil.
- **Open chat** sits where Open does, on a bot's entry or run that left a block in the chat (`starting`, `answered` or `failed`; a routine by its newest run), and the row's click does the same (`Click::Chat`): the sheet closes and the chat shows at that run's block. A missed run and a pending reminder left none, so they offer nothing.
- **`answered`** is a state of its own (`State::Answered`): under Started, `Answered` in the done colour with the done icon, clearable as the server's `clearable` counts it. A bot's `starting` reads `Answering…`.
- **Its place reads `bot prs`** (the id, the issue's and `queue list`'s words; `place_label` has no list of bots to name it).
- **Remove's tooltip** on a bot's routine says that what it answered stays in the chat, and on a run or a one-off that ran that its answer does.
- **The sheet scoped to a bot** (`QueuePanel::scope`, from `Store::routines_of`, titled `Routines › PRs`): only its entries, the filter and Schedule… in the toolbar, no Clear started (the request clears every project's too), no empty Scheduled section, its own Routines note and empty page. A removal's Undo shows only in the scope it was asked in (`undo::undo_shown`), so a reply that comes back after the sheet moved to another bot or to the projects' queue offers nothing. A change of scope drops the form, the detail, the undo bar and the filter's text.
- **Clear started still clears the bots' runs, and says so.** The projects' sheet offers it as before (when one of its own entries is clearable), and it sends `queue clear-started --json`, which clears every clearable entry, the bots' answered and failed runs included: the request has no way to leave them (usecornercase/cornercase-terminal#929), while upstream's own window does it with an internal `clear_started_where(|e| !e.is_bot())`. So its tooltip says `…, your bots' runs too (leaving those needs usecornercase/cornercase-terminal#929)` while any is clearable (`bot::cleared_too`), and the undo bar says `Removed 5 started entries, 2 of them your bots' runs` (`1 of them a bot's run`), so what went unseen is said and Undo is in reach. Rejected: one `queue remove` per entry (a copy of the server's rule of what is clearable, and one undo number per entry against the 16 the server keeps, so Undo breaks past 16), resting the button while a bot has a finished run (anyone with a daily bot routine would lose it for good), and a question first (removing asks nothing here, and Undo covers it).
- **A queue message is told while a bot's sheet shows** (`notify::telling::Looking`): only the projects' sheet swallows them (`design/notifications.md`), since the bot's sheet shows no project's entry. A `queue-failed` toast of a bot's run still says Open Queue, which no longer shows that run; its sentence names the bot and the routine, and opening the bot needs the entry on the message (usecornercase/cornercase-terminal#930). Rejected: telling a bot's failed run through the bot's own toast instead (the queue's message cannot be recognised as the same failure, so it would be told twice).
- **What of upstream's #896 this covers**: nothing new. This sheet has been a modal of its own, with each routine's runs under it and a prompt of several lines, since before #896. **Not covered: folding a routine's runs, and upstream's order by next run (#386)**: nothing of it fell out of this change, since folding needs a state per routine and a control per group that none of this adds.

## The sheet takes the keys

Part 2 of #267 made the Queue sheet the place that has the keys (`design/widgets.md`, A button takes the keys), so its new buttons are reached with Tab and pressed with Return or Space.

- **Where the keys go** (`keys::keys_go`): to the form's prompt while the form is open, else to the filter while the list or "no match" shows, else to the page's first button on an empty page (Schedule an agent… on the projects', Schedule a routine… on a bot's), and nowhere while the queue loads (the flag waits for the next frame). They go there when the sheet opens, when its scope changes, when the form closes (Cancel, a save, an entry that left), and when the filter or the page's button that had them leaves the frame for the other (`keys::follows`: the last entry removed, an Undo that brings it back; found live, since a handle that is no longer drawn keeps the focus and the keys then reached nothing), through `cx.defer_in`, so the handle is in the frame just drawn (`terminal.rs`'s `keep_the_keys` does the same). Before, the sheet never took the focus, so its keys stayed on `Root`'s handle or the chat's field, whose stops are off under a sheet, and `Root::on_key` drops Tab under an overlay: a stop in it was unreachable. The projects' queue takes them too, so typing filters as soon as it opens, as in Issues and Settings.

## Results

`queue show ID --json` (cornercase 0.13.51, feature `queue-results`) answers an entry with its result, and `--conversation` the messages. The released contract (v0.13.53's `design/queue.md`, "Results") is what this follows; the issue's text, written before the release, is not.

- **Where it lives: a page in the sheet's right column**, the one the schedule form uses (`SIDE`, one draggable width). The column holds the form or the detail, never both. Rejected: a dialog over the sheet (capped at 560 px, too short for a conversation, closed to look at the next run, and a dialog over a sheet leaves the focus on a gone field) and a whole-sheet page (hides the list you move along). Escape stays `Root`'s. When the column closes (✕, or its entry leaving the feed) and the detail held the focus, the focus goes to the filter field, since the detail's handle is no longer drawn.
- **Which rows reach it**: a click on a started or kept entry whose pane is gone, a removed run, and a failed or missed run (`data::offers_detail`); a failed or missed one-off still opens its form. A row with a live pane keeps Open on its click and reaches the detail through its message icon, only once the feed gives it `result_at`, since before that the note would say no result was kept while the agent still works.
- **The gate is the server's release and this computer's command line** (`cli::Feature::QueueResults`, 0.13.51, asked by `result::results_offered` of the `cli::Releases` that `Store::releases()` gives, `design/connection.md`: the order and the unread and unreadable cases are there), decided once per render in `Snapshot::results`. Rejected: reading `contract`'s features, a second way to gate and one more command. Below the server's gate nothing is offered and no line says so, so every row reads and acts as before; the README and the changelog name the release. This computer's command line parses `queue show` too, so one older than 0.13.51 against a newer server refused it after the press (#279); `cli::MINIMUM` is unchanged.
  - **A line says it, only for the command line**: above the list, `Showing what a run did needs cornercase 0.13.51 or newer on this Mac (it has 0.13.50).` (`result::results_note`), without the parenthesis when the release could not be read. It shows only when a drawn row would reach the detail through its click or its message icon (`data::shows_results_note`, which asks `offers_result` of each row and run, a live pane without a result included), so it never talks about rows that open nothing. The server's silence does not carry over: an older server keeps no result, so nothing is missing, while a newer one keeps them and this computer cannot show them. An open detail closes by itself once the command line turns out older, as it does for the server.
- **Checked live** on 2026-11-14, macOS on Apple silicon, a debug build of #279's change, `--remote` to a private 0.13.76 server: with 0.13.27 or 0.13.50 as this computer's command line the line stood above the list for a started entry whose pane had closed, and with 0.13.63 and 0.13.76 it was absent (the UI reviewer's pass), with no error in the window's log; the first pass saw it above an entry with a live pane and no result, the case the line now leaves out, and on the corrected build an entry with a live pane and no result showed no line and the line came once its pane closed. A row's detail was not opened (no press could be posted).
- **When it asks**: on opening and when that entry's `result_at` in the feed changes; the conversation only when chosen (or chosen again after a partial answer, below), and from then on every read carries it (`read::Reads`). Each ask has a number, taken from a counter of the panel so that numbers never repeat across openings of an entry, and an answer counts only above the one shown and above the opening's first, so a late older answer never replaces a newer one; a plain answer keeps the conversation shown. No stream, no polling.
- **Jobs and exit 3**: a read is a job beside the queue (`Reading what “…” did…`, `Reading the conversation of “…”…`), parsed off the main thread (`result::Answer::read`). Exit 3 is an answer: the last message is kept and the server's note says why. With `result.conversation` false (the record is gone) Conversation is no longer offered; with it true (the record is there but its conversation could not be read, `messages: null`) Conversation stays and choosing it asks again, showing the loading line meanwhile (`read::Reads::wants_talk`, `result::can_retry_talk`). A failure is the detail's red line with Try again, never a toast; a conversation read shows the busy mark only over a conversation already shown (`read::refreshing_keys`); a read that lands after the detail moved on is dropped.
- **What it shows**: the facts (Scheduled, Started, Result, Agent, the pull request as `pull_tag` draws it and linked when its URL is a web one, as it was when the result was kept, then Branch; `result::facts` gives them in that order), then the server's note as it words it. The window decides nothing from the note: the booleans `conversation`, `cut` and `earlier` do. Last message and conversation are Markdown (links open only web addresses).
- **Select and copy**: Rendered is not selectable (only a bot's chat selects Markdown today, `design/bots.md`; #394); Text draws the same content as lines with `ui/selection.rs`. Copy copies all, ⌘C the selection while the sheet is open, and each message has a copy icon. Tabs are drawn as spaces in Text, so a copy has spaces, and the header's Copy copies the displayed lines in Text and the raw message in Rendered. What a selection copies follows the viewer's rule (`design/files-panel.md`, Selecting in the viewer), a line break before its end included, and a selected line break is drawn as the viewer's (`design/files-panel.md`, Selecting in the viewer). Switching page, view or entry clears the selection.
- **Long conversations** (up to 1,000 messages of 32 KiB) are gpui `list`s, one per page and view, and Markdown is parsed in the job. The whole parsed conversation stays in memory while shown (tens of MB at the caps) and goes when the detail closes or moves on.
- **A flow run's result is its orchestrator's; a custom agent leaves no record**, so it has no message (the note says why).
- **Frame times** (2026-11-13, after the last change to the conversation page; a debug build, whose dependencies are optimised, on a loaded Mac, window 900×640, `ZED_MEASUREMENTS=1`, a conversation of 1,000 messages, three scroll runs of six wheel steps each): the median frame while scrolling was 4.5, 4.5 and 5.5 ms in Rendered and 4.5, 4.6 and 4.5 ms in Text, the slowest 8.3 ms. - **A label stops at three lines, with no "…"**: gpui's `text_ellipsis` with `line_clamp(3)` cuts the text as one unwrapped line to three widths and wraps it again, so the "…" can fall on a fourth line that is dropped (so the Issues title is no better, by luck of length). Open, Copy and ✕ sit beside it, outside the scrolling head; the pull request comes before the branch, since the head (capped at 40 % of the column) can hide its last rows. Copy is offered only when there is text (`result::can_copy`), without building the conversation's text on each frame.
