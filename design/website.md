# Website

Code: `site/`, `.github/workflows/pages.yml`, `tests/website.rs`

The landing page for this window, asked for on 2026-11-05 ("ultra mega impactante, full motion, only a landing", as cornercase has at `usecornercase.dev`). One page, in English, no docs: the docs stay on `usecornercase.dev`.

## Stack and hosting

- **Astro, as cornercase's own site**, so both repositories build alike, with no framework on the client. The animation has **no runtime dependency**: what the page needs (a value per frame from the scroll) is small. `lib/` holds the plain logic, tested without a browser (`npm test`, `node:test` bundled with esbuild); `client/` the DOM and WebGL.
- **Every URL is relative** (`assetsPrefix: '.'`, stylesheets inlined, so a font's `url()` is relative to the page and not to a CSS file). The same build works at `usecornercase.github.io/cornercase-gpui/` and at `gui.usecornercase.dev/`, so a change of domain breaks nothing while it settles; cornercase's site uses an absolute `SITE_BASE`, which breaks the assets until the next build. Only the canonical and `og:` addresses take `SITE_ORIGIN` and `SITE_BASE` from `actions/configure-pages`, so they catch up at the next deployment: run the Pages workflow by hand after changing the domain.
- **The domain** is `gui.usecornercase.dev`: a `CNAME` from `gui` to `usecornercase.github.io` at DigitalOcean, where `usecornercase.dev` is (cornercase's `design/website.md`), and the custom domain in this repository's Settings › Pages. A deployment from Actions needs no `CNAME` file. GitHub redirects the github.io address once it is set.
- **`og.png`** is a shot of the page's hero at 1200×630, made by hand with a headless Chrome: retake it when the hero's words change.
- **A capture leaves a headless Chrome behind** when the Browser panel opened and the app is stopped with `kill`, as AGENTS.md asks: the panel ends it only on a normal quit. Read its pid (the number after the last `-` of `readlink` of the `SingletonLock` in the profile under the fake home), look at it with `ps -o pid=,command= -p <pid>`, and end it by that pid (`design/manual-check.md`, Cleaning up).

## The look

- **This app's look, not `usecornercase.dev`'s**: the same colours, type and mark as that site (cornercase's `design/website.md`, "The brand"), with this window's glass, rounded shapes and cobalt glow instead of its square, flat surfaces, which it keeps for a page of its own that reads as documentation. The page is asked to be spectacular and its subject is how this window looks, so it takes the window's language (`design/widgets.md`).

## The field

- **One fluid simulation behind the whole page, drawn in terminal cells** (`client/field/`, WebGL2): Navier-Stokes on a small grid (about 90 rows of velocity, one dye texel per cell), and a full-screen pass that picks a glyph per cell from an atlas drawn with JetBrains Mono, by the dye's density or, where it runs fast, by its direction (`─ ╱ │ ╲`). The product is a terminal drawn on the GPU, so the page's ground is too. Rejected: a video, which does not answer the pointer; a noise shader alone, which has no currents to push.
- **Five walkers keep it alive without a pointer**, the pointer pushes it, the scroll's speed drifts it, and a status changes its colour in Know who needs you, with a burst at each beat. Colours are the app's: cobalt, and yellow, orange, red and green only for working, waiting, failed and done.
- **The mark is drawn in cells by the same pass** (`markOn`, its 12-unit grid in the shader): it assembles at load, is stamped into the dye and blown away, and assembles again above the final title. Cells fray at random, so it reads as printed by a terminal.
- **It can give up**: at 38 fps or less for 1.5 s it lowers the pressure iterations, then drops to one device pixel and removes the window's backdrop blur (`.lite`), the costliest thing on the page. No WebGL2 or no renderable float texture falls back to the still page. A lost context (a phone that put the tab away) pauses it, and it is rebuilt when the context comes back.
- **The walkers belong to the simulation's steps**, not to the display's frames, scaled by the time since the last step, so a 120 Hz screen has the same currents as a 60 Hz one.

## The window

- **Drawn in HTML at the app's real size** (`components/stage/`, 1280×800, the app's default), from `src/theme.rs`'s colours, `assets/icons/` and the measures of a real capture, then moved as one object by a camera. Vector text stays sharp at any zoom, and glass shows the live field through it, which a picture could not do. It is an illustration, not a simulation: the terminal's own site simulates its app, at the cost of a rule to keep them in step, and this page shows one scripted story; what of it a test keeps in step with the app is under Kept in step with the app. The cast is the README screenshot's and cornercase's site's (web-shop, dark mode, gift cards, the empty address), so all the pictures tell one session.
- **A camera, not a layout, makes it responsive** (`lib/track.ts`): each shot names a rectangle of the window and the side left free for the caption, and the pose is worked out for the viewport. On a phone the same window is framed tighter above a caption sheet, so the text in it stays readable instead of a 390 px wide miniature.
- **Very large screens** scale the captions (`zoom`, from 1900 px wide) and let the camera use the room; a phone on its side (under 480 px tall) drops what does not fit, not the headings.
- **Everything is a function of the scroll**, smoothed once (`client/scroll.ts`), and writes are cached (`client/paint.ts`), so scrolling back, a jump to an anchor and a reload midway need no special code. Native scrolling stays: the page does not hijack the wheel. Short state changes (a toast, a dock page) are CSS transitions set by a class, at the app's own 180 ms in, 120 ms out.
- **What is simplified**: panes that do not fit beside the dock stack instead of shrinking; the conversation's look follows Claude Code as cornercase's simulated agent does.

## Kept in step with the app

- **The rail and the docked pages' heads are compared with the app's by a test** (`tests/website.rs`, #240, 2026-11-14). The page's rail went eight days without Machines (#40): the change that put it in the app added its sheet to the page's ring but not to its rail (`Rail.astro`), and nothing compared the two rails.
  - It reads as text the app's rail (`src/sidebar/rail.rs`: its two lists, the order `column` draws them in, where its rule sits and what `render_rail` puts after it; Help's icon, `tours_button`, and the gear's, `rail/gear.rs`), each panel's icon (`src/panels/mod.rs`) and each icon's file (`src/ui/icons.rs`), and the page's (`components/stage/Rail.astro`: its three lists, its rule and its gap). Order counts, not only membership; it holds no list of icons of its own.
  - It fails when an icon is added, removed, swapped or changed on either side, and when a `Dock*.astro` heads its page with another icon or title than its panel's (the panel is the file's name: `DockFlow.astro` is `PanelKind::Flow`) (`PanelKind::icon` and `title`, as `src/app.rs`'s `panel_title` draws them). The message names the icon, the side and the file to change.
  - It fails rather than passes when it meets a shape it cannot read (a function it cannot find, a call in `column` or a `.children(` in `render_rail` it does not know, an icon with no file, an `<Icon` on the page it cannot place), and the message says to teach the test the new shape; the test file holds the full list. A change to the rail's code that keeps what it draws can fail it too. A constant renamed, or an array written on other lines, passes.
- **It compares a flow's workspace with no plugin's button.** The page's markup holds Flow's icon after the Browser, which `stage.css` shows only in the story's Flow chapter, so the markup is a flow's workspace's rail and contains the other. A plugin's button is the person's, not the app's, and the story has none.
- **It is in Rust under `tests/`, reading both sides as text as `tests/layers.rs` does**, so it is one of the required checks of every pull request, on the three systems, and runs on the change that starts a drift, from either side. Rejected: a test beside the rail in `src/`, which could call `column` instead of reading it, but a change under `src/` is a change to the app, so a version and a release that tells users nothing; a test in `site/tests/` like `copy.test.ts`, which runs only when `pages.yml`'s paths match, is not a check `main` requires, and does not run on a change to the app; a parser crate for the Rust side, which is a change to `Cargo.toml`, so a release too. The page's lists stay in `Rail.astro`: moving them to a data file would change the page for nothing a Rust test needs.
- **`site/tests/copy.test.ts` checks** the sheets' keys against `src/keymap.rs`, their names against `src/panels/mod.rs`, the cornercase version the page asks for against `src/cli/locate.rs` and the downloads against the README; `pages.yml` runs it when one of those files changes. A sheet the app adds and the ring lacks fails nothing: its picture is a capture retaken by hand (#71), and a check would hold every pull request until one is taken.
- **Kept true by hand**, under `AGENTS.md`'s rule that a change to a feature or a key updates the page: the labels, the captions' keys (`src/keymap.rs`'s own tests pin every panel's key), statuses, the Browser's picked card and its "Reloaded: claude finished", and the Flow panel's plan. They are words and numbers placed one by one in markup, so a test would need a third list saying what each is; the rail is a list on both sides. The Browser's pick banner never followed the app's words (#271).
- **An illustration on purpose, not compared**: the sidebar's rows and marks and the panes' text (the story's cast), which docked pages the story opens (no Recap) and their bodies, the status bar, the colours (the default dark theme's, written as CSS tokens, in the field's shader and in gradients, so a check of one form would claim more than it holds) and the measures, the panel widths among them (a real capture's, framed by a camera).

## Real captures

- **The sheets' ring shows real screens, not drawings** (`src/assets/sheets/`), and so do the still pictures (`src/assets/stills/`): the debug build on a private server, with cornercase's `site/capture/` fake home (its projects, agents, `gh`), `CORNERCASE_GPUI_OPEN=<panel>`, a dark window behind the app for its blur to show, and `screencapture -R` of the window's rectangle, since `-l` composes the window over grey without its blur. They were taken on 2026-11-05 with cornercase 0.13.0; retake them when a sheet changes much. Paths in them are the fake home's. Machines' (2026-11-06, cornercase 0.13.1) is the one taken elsewhere: the Linux build under Xvfb, so without a desktop's blur behind it, with `machines` answered from a file so that it shows every state; retake it on a Mac with the others.
- **Why a ring you stand inside**: twelve sheets one key apart read as one room, and the neighbours wrapping around the current one say there are more. A grid would be a wall of small screens, and it is what the page falls back to without motion.

## Without motion

- **The plain page comes first**: without JavaScript, with reduced motion (also if it is turned on with the page open), without WebGL2, on a screen under 300 px tall, if the script has not run after 4 s or if it throws, the sections flow as ordinary text with real pictures (`html:not(.motion)`). The inline script adds `.motion` before the first paint, so the show has no flash of the plain page. Without JavaScript the install box lists all three commands.
- **A screen reader reads the same page in both**: the window, the field and the ring are `aria-hidden`; captions fade with `opacity`, never `visibility`, so they stay in the tree outside their chapter; the sheets are also a visually hidden list, since the ring only shows one at a time.
- **`.motion` keeps the semantic order**: titles are split in lines and letters only inside `aria-hidden` spans, with the whole text as the heading's `aria-label`.

## What the page claims

- Keep claims to what the README and these notes back: GPUI, the platforms and their requirements, signing, updates. The download's size and version come from GitHub's API at load, and the page says nothing of them if that fails. Nothing is compared with another app, and no frame rate is promised: the counter in the corner measures the visitor's own page.
- A visitor on Linux or Windows reads the keys as they are there (`lib/os.ts`, the README's rules).
- The landing is in English only (`AGENTS.md`), unlike cornercase's, which also has Spanish and Brazilian Portuguese.

## Checked

Checked live on 2026-11-05 on an M5, headless, with Playwright: Chrome (GPU through ANGLE Metal) at 1440×900 and 390×844, every section at several scroll positions, the intro frame by frame, the built page served under `/cornercase-gpui/`, a reload midway, the anchors, reduced motion and no JavaScript; a whole scroll with the wheel held 60 fps, the most headless Chrome draws, with a worst frame of 33 ms in three runs and a single one of 150 ms in a fourth; a lost context restored, reduced motion turned on with the page open, a denied clipboard; also 2560×1440, 1100×700, 844×390 and 820×1180. WebKit and Firefox at 1440×900, every section: WebKit ignored a perspective set two levels up and drew the ring from outside until each card took its own (`Sheets::placeCards`); headless Firefox ran slowly enough to fall back to `.lite`, which also proved the fallback. Not checked: Safari itself, a real phone, or a weak GPU.
