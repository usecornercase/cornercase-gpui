# Sidebar

Code: `src/sidebar.rs`, `src/sidebar/`

- **Where the code is**: `sidebar.rs` holds the shared types (`Target`, `Dragged`, `Rename`, `SideState`), the sidebar's frame; `sidebar/key.rs` the plain key of what the sidebar draws, which decides when its cached part is redrawn (`design/widgets.md`); `sidebar/rail/key.rs` the rail's (`RailKey`); `sidebar/look.rs` the settings of cornercase's that the window follows; `sidebar/arrange.rs` the cards and lists of each arrangement; `sidebar/tab_rows.rs` the tab rows; `sidebar/agents.rs` the agents section; `sidebar/machine_agents.rs` a machine's rows in it; `sidebar/rows.rs` the group and project rows and what every row shares (its end and hover buttons, the hovered row, menu, rename in place, drop rules); `sidebar/workspace.rs` the workspace and agent rows; `sidebar/menus.rs` the right-click menus; `sidebar/actions.rs` folding, renaming and dropping; `sidebar/opening.rs` opening a project and importing a VS Code workspace; `sidebar/order.rs` the drop rules as plain functions, tested without a window (the order itself, `Session::sections`, and an agent's title, `PaneInfo::agent_title`, are in `model.rs`, shared with Search); `sidebar/forms.rs` and its folder the dialogs (new group, icon and colour, delete group, close agent; Remove worktree… asks the Worktrees sheet's question, `panels/worktrees/row_removal.rs`).
- **Panels open from a rail of icons** (`sidebar/rail.rs`), a narrow card left of the sidebar's, as in Xcode: the docked panels (Changes, Files, TODO, Recap, the Browser, and Flow while the shown workspace runs a flow) on top, then the plugins' buttons (`design/plugins.md`), the sheets below a divider, Settings at the bottom. Each icon is tinted while its panel is open and its tooltip names the panel and its keys. The panels' icons scroll when the window is too short for them, so Help, the keys and Settings always show: with Recap's icon, the window's opening size, 1280 × 800, no longer fits them all in a flow's workspace. With the twelfth sheet, Machines, the buttons went from 6 to 4 px apart, which fits them all at that size outside a flow's workspace. It replaced a wrap of text buttons under the project list and the dock toggles in the status bar: two places for one job, both easy to miss, and the list lost room to them. The rail was chosen over a section of rows in the sidebar (Finder style) because eleven rows would push the projects down. The rail is a cached part (#115, `design/widgets.md`).
- **The rail's icons say what waits, as the TUI's icon bar does** (its `design/layout/tab-bar-and-icon-bar.md`, Indicators): Changes shows how many files the shown workspace changed in a small disc at the icon's corner (`9+` from ten; the exact count is in its tooltip), from the workspace's `changes` in `view`, and TODO a grey dot while items are left to do (`todo_dot` in Settings › UI, from the app feed's `todos`). A disc opaque over the icon, since the corner of Changes' icon is under it. The TUI's green dot on its gear, while a browser is attached or you are away on a chat app, is left out: the window has no gear that holds those, and the Settings icon opens one sheet.
- **Look** (`design/widgets.md`): the rail and the list are two floating cards (`card`), 8 px apart and in from the window's edges, each a layer of its own as the panes and the dock are (asked for on 2026-10-26; until then they shared one card, as macOS 26 floats a sidebar with the traffic lights in its corner). Both start below the title row, level with the panes: the traffic lights span 16 to 76 px on macOS 26, wider than the rail's 44 px card, and putting them in the list's card would move them out of the window's corner, past the rail. So they and the title strip (the mark and the wordmark) sit on the ground above the cards. Linux has no traffic lights (`design/window.md`), so there the title strip reaches back over the rail's column to the window's edge and the mark takes their corner, centred over the rail's icons, with the wordmark 8 px after it as on a Mac (`mark_lead`, 2026-11-04); leaving their 76 px empty looked like a missing control, and the mark alone over the rail with the wordmark over the list split the lockup by 22 px. The strip, with the tab row, is the title bar that moves the window there. While a docked panel takes the sidebar's place in a narrow window (`design/window.md`), the mark stays alone over the rail on Linux and Windows (`corner`, built from `mark_lead`), and on a Mac the tab row starts where the mark did (`tabs_lead`). A cobalt wash lights the top of each card. The rail's first icon is level with the "Projects" header and its last with the footer's buttons, so the two cards read as a pair. In a window too short for the rail, its card runs past the window's bottom rather than letting the icons spill out of it; the list's card shrinks and its rows scroll. The selected workspace and agent are a rounded cobalt tint, as macOS fills a selected sidebar row; the glowing bar and gradient they had went with the site's square shapes. Only the deepest shown row is tinted: when the shown workspace lists its tabs (or its shown agent), the tab is lit and the workspace only takes the brighter, medium-weight name, so two cobalt blocks do not stack (asked for on 2026-10-29, with "everything is too big" and "the nesting does not show"). Children hang from a thin guide line under their parent's chevron (`rows::nested`), workspaces under a project in the tree and tabs or agents under a workspace, since an indent alone did not read as nesting. The lists are 12.5 px with a 1.4 line height (gpui's default, 1.618, made every row about 30 px tall), project names medium rather than semibold, a tab is one line whose details are left out when they repeat its title (a `zsh` tab running zsh), and a workspace counts its tabs only while folded or when its tabs are not listed under it. Names not selected are in the second grey, details in the third. The section title is "Projects" in title case, and the footer's buttons carry icons, "Agent" in cobalt as the main action.
- **`SideState` is shared with the tab bar and the panes**: a menu, a rename and a right press on a pane (a menu on release, or a move onto another pane) belong to the window, not to one row, so they live on `Root`.
- **`Target` names a row of the sidebar or of the tab bar**, so menus, renames and drags work the same way for tabs; its `flag` is how cornercase's commands name it (`--group`, `--project`, `--workspace`, `--tab`).
- **`cmd` is `store::args`** under the name the window's files already used; it was a copy of it.
- **The list is a cached view** (#115, `design/widgets.md`): `Root::sidebar_part` builds the cards for `app/part.rs`'s `Part`, which gpui redraws only when it or something in it is notified, and `Root` notifies it when `SidebarKey` moves. The menu is drawn by `Root`, not the part, since it is deferred and anchored to the window; the machine rows' countdown (`reconnecting in 4 s`, `seen 2 min ago`) is the part's own `Counting`, set by `Root::parts.counted`; the working orbits are drawn by `OrbitLayer` over the part, so a row's badge is `ui::orbits::badge`, which `machine_rows::status_of` shares with the tab bar. The rail, the tab strip and the status bar are parts too. `tests/layers.rs` checks that the sidebar reads only what its key holds (`design/code-layout.md`).
- **Row buttons appear only on hover, just before the row's figures** (`rows::trailing`, the row's end: a workspace's pull request tags, `⇄N`, `↓N` and status, a project's count and status, a group's count and chevron, a folded agent's `+N`), or at the row's end when it has none, so they take no room from the name the rest of the time and never cover what the row says. `slot` counts leftwards from there. They are opaque rounded chips (`raised`), centred on the line the figures sit on (a workspace's first line): over a translucent row, the name would show through a translucent button. While a row is hovered its name makes room for them and ends in its ellipsis a gap before them instead of running under them; the figures never move, so the pull request tag stays under a press even if the room goes during it (gpui stops reporting a pressed row as hovered once the pointer moves). At rest an end without figures gives its gap back to the name, so a row looks as it did before the pointer came. Since #115 the buttons are built only on the hovered row (`row_hovered`), so a frame lays out none of them for the other rows; after a scroll with the pointer still, the row now under it shows its buttons once the pointer moves, and while a row is held down and the pointer moves gpui reports it unhovered, so its buttons go until the press ends (a button acts on press, so one pressed never goes before it acts). Until 2026-11-01 the buttons sat at the row's right end, over the figures: hovering a workspace hid its pull request tag (asked to fix on that day), and other rows their count, status, chevron or `+N`.
  - **The room follows `on_hover`, the chips a paint-time `group_hover`**: gpui applies hover styles only when painting (`design/widgets.md`), so a margin set under hover would not move anything. `SideState::hovered` keeps the row under the pointer; leaving a row clears it only while it is still the hovered one (`after_hover`), since one mouse move can report the next row first. It follows the pointer's moves, so after a scroll with the pointer still, the row now under it shows nothing (its buttons are not built) until the pointer moves, and the one hovered before keeps its room, its built buttons hidden by `group_hover`.
  - Rejected: reserving the buttons' room on every row at all times, as the TUI does (`room` in its `ui/rows.rs`), which costs every name 26 px (50 px for a project's two buttons) for buttons shown on one row at a time; sliding the figures left to make room, since what the eye reads would jump on every row the pointer crosses and a tag could move from under a press; hiding the figures under the buttons while hovered, as Zed's list items swap their end, which hides the pull request tag and its click.

- **A fold shows at once** (`sidebar/folds.rs`, a stopgap until #707 upstream): the view's lines reach the window about 500 ms after any command, since the command's own connection opens the server's pacing window (measured on 0.12.225: the command answers in 5 to 14 ms, the line follows at 501 to 517 ms, every time, idle or not). #549 reported it and was closed on 2026-10-29 with nothing changed, so it was measured again on 2026-11-07 before deleting anything (cornercase 0.13.19, Linux x86_64, a private server; no macOS, no Windows): over 48 folds on an idle server, 1 s apart, 2 s apart and back to back, on `view` and on `status --follow`, the command answers in 7 to 27 ms and the line follows at 479 to 504 ms. With this window's view and three flow runs open, 12 folds gave a median of 492 ms and a lowest of 46 ms, since a pacing window opened earlier by something else ends sooner. A click should show within about 100 ms not to feel late, so this stays until #707 lands. So a click on a chevron or a Collapse/Expand item flips the flag in the window's copy of the session, and each session that arrives gets it again until one agrees or the command has answered; from then on the server's word stands, so a fold changed elsewhere in the meantime is not overridden for good. A failed command flips it back and toasts. Rejected: flipping it with no pending list, since a session sent before the command arrived would unfold the row for half a second. Other actions read back from the session (rename, drag and drop) still wait; once #707 lands this goes.
- **A row's click focuses it, a double click renames it in place**, a right click or its `⋯` opens its menu. A group's click folds it instead.
- **Renaming**: enter saves, escape or a click elsewhere cancels. A group needs a name, so an empty one is not sent; a project, a workspace or a tab goes back to its default name (folder, branch, program) when emptied, as the placeholders say.
- **Drag and drop** (`cornercase move`): a row dropped on a sibling takes its place, so it lands after the target when it came from above (as the TUI's drag does) and before it otherwise. The end of a list is `--last`, or for a project `--into GROUP` (or `--ungroup` outside any group). Workspaces stay in their project and tabs in their workspace. A project dropped on a group goes into it; a group dropped on a project moves next to that project's group. The empty space below the rows takes a project out of its group, or a group to the end.
- **A drag's preview is one line of at most 240 px** (#167, 2026-11-13). `DragPreview` (a tab, and a project, group, workspace or tab row) held its label as a plain text: gpui 0.2.2 lays a drag's view out at `AvailableSpace::min_size()`, where a text is its whole line, so an 817-character tab name made a chip as wide as the window. It now holds `ui::one_line(label).widest(240)`: `OneLine::widest` gives the line its whole width up to that, also at the least size, where a plain `one_line` measures nothing, and the text ends in "…". 240 px (about 38 characters at the preview's 12 px) is a little more than a tab shows (200 px) or a row does; any value from 200 to 280 reads the same. A short name's chip is as wide as the name. Rejected: a wrap under a widest width, as the TODO panel's preview does (a name of 817 characters made 16 lines hanging from the pointer, where tabs and rows show one); `whitespace_nowrap` with `max_w` (cuts mid-letter, `design/widgets.md`). The TODO panel's own preview is capped at 360 pt and wraps inside it at gpui's least size, which was seen before the change, so it keeps it. Checked live on 2026-11-13 (a debug build of 0.1.73, `manual-check drag … hold 1500`, a capture 0.9 s in): the 817-character tab and the 115-character workspace row show one chip ending in "…" about 262 pt wide, the `api` tab a small chip, and a tab dropped on another and the row still moved. The project row's chip and a drop into a group worked, the group row's chip showed and its drop on a project that is in no group changed nothing, as before; the TODO panel's preview still wraps within 360 pt. A drag that starts inside an inline rename's field selects text and lifts neither the tab nor the row (`design/widgets.md`).

- **Importing a VS Code workspace** (`sidebar/opening.rs`, `open FILE --focus --json`, cornercase 0.13.16): **Import VS Code workspace…** in the Projects `+`, and in a group's menu after Add project…, with `--into GROUP`. It is an entry of its own, where the TUI's picker lists folders and `.code-workspace` files together, because gpui's `prompt_for_paths` cannot offer both: on Linux the portal's `directory` is one switch and on Windows `FOS_PICKFOLDERS` is too, only macOS mixes them. A picked path that is not UTF-8 is refused in a toast rather than altered, since arguments go to the command as text and a replaced byte could name another file.
  - **What it says** is the TUI's picker's toast, word for word (`Imported::message`, from `missing` and `failed` of the answer): `2 projects imported into team`, `2 projects imported into team, 1 not found` (`missing`: a folder that is gone), `1 project imported into team, 1 could not open, see server.log` (`failed`: a folder that could not start a shell) and `nothing imported from team, 1 not found`. Exit 3 is that answer, not a failure (`store::run_partial_busy`, which takes 0 and 3 and reads the JSON); any other exit is the refusal, in a toast: a file that does not parse, one that is not there, a group that is gone, an older server. A toast is green unless a folder failed or nothing went in: the TUI's bug icon marks only `failed`, but an import that did nothing is no success here, where the toast is the only thing that changes.

- **Run setup** (`setup --workspace ID --json --focus`, cornercase 0.13.13; its job key, `store::setup_key`, lets the notifier skip the server's `setup-failed` for that workspace while the job runs, `design/notifications.md`), where the TUI's menu has **run setup**, and offered when it offers it: a worktree (`worktree`) not already running its setup (`setup`) of a project with a setup (the `setup` of `projects` in `config --json`; `Look::has_setup`, `sidebar/look.rs`). cornercase's own note says those three decide it (its `design/worktree-setup.md`, Offering it). The window already reads `config --json` when the session's `config.revision` grows, and setting a project's script grows it, so reading it for this costs no extra call and a project's menu follows Settings. A setup not yet read leaves the entry out for that moment. The command answers when the setup is done, however long it takes, so it runs beside the queue as a job (`store::run_busy`, the status bar's `Running the setup of …`); `--focus` shows its new tab at once, as the TUI's click does, and the row says `setup…`. A refusal (a second run, a project that lost its setup) or a setup that failed is the toast, in cornercase's words, naming the status and the pane; success says nothing, as in the TUI, since the row's `setup…` going away and the tab turning into a shell already say it.
  - **Which entry is a project's**: an entry names it by `project` (its id), by `folder`, or by `key` with the server's home expanded, any of them, and a blank setup counts for nothing. No one of them is enough. A project opened after `config --json` was read has no id in it, and opening one does not grow the revision. A project whose folder was moved and replaced by a symlink while open gets two entries, as the bench's server printed: the script under its old `key`, with `project` null and the new `folder`, and a blank entry with its id and old folder, while the server still runs the setup (it matches the raw key, `config::names_folder`). So asking for an exact identity would hide it. Paths compare with `paths::same_path`, the comparison `home_relative` already used: exact on Unix, and on Windows across separators and case, which is looser than the server beyond the drive letter. That is the safe way to be wrong: a wrong entry gets the server's refusal in a toast, while a wrong hide leaves no way to retry.
