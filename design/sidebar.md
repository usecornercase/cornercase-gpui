# Sidebar

Code: `src/sidebar.rs`, `src/sidebar/`

- **Where the code is**: `sidebar.rs` holds the shared types (`Target`, `Dragged`, `Rename`, `SideState`), the sidebar's frame; `sidebar/look.rs` the settings of cornercase's that the window follows; `sidebar/arrange.rs` the cards and lists of each arrangement; `sidebar/tab_rows.rs` the tab rows; `sidebar/agents.rs` the agents section; `sidebar/rows.rs` the group and project rows and what every row shares (its end and hover buttons, the hovered row, menu, rename in place, drop rules); `sidebar/workspace.rs` the workspace and agent rows; `sidebar/menus.rs` the right-click menus; `sidebar/actions.rs` folding, renaming, dropping and opening projects; `sidebar/order.rs` the drop rules as plain functions, tested without a window (the order itself, `Session::sections`, and an agent's title, `PaneInfo::agent_title`, are in `model.rs`, shared with Search); `sidebar/forms.rs` and its folder the dialogs (new group, icon and colour, delete group, remove worktree, close agent).
- **Panels open from a rail of icons** (`sidebar/rail.rs`), a narrow card left of the sidebar's, as in Xcode: the docked panels (Changes, Files, TODO, Recap, the Browser, and Flow while the shown workspace runs a flow) on top, then the plugins' buttons (`design/plugins.md`), the sheets below a divider, Settings at the bottom. Each icon is tinted while its panel is open and its tooltip names the panel and its keys. The panels' icons scroll when the window is too short for them, so Help, the keys and Settings always show: with Recap's icon, the window's opening size, 1280 × 800, no longer fits them all in a flow's workspace. With the twelfth sheet, Machines, the buttons went from 6 to 4 px apart, which fits them all at that size outside a flow's workspace. It replaced a wrap of text buttons under the project list and the dock toggles in the status bar: two places for one job, both easy to miss, and the list lost room to them. The rail was chosen over a section of rows in the sidebar (Finder style) because eleven rows would push the projects down.
- **The rail's icons say what waits, as the TUI's icon bar does** (its `design/layout/tab-bar-and-icon-bar.md`, Indicators): Changes shows how many files the shown workspace changed in a small disc at the icon's corner (`9+` from ten; the exact count is in its tooltip), from the workspace's `changes` in `view`, and TODO a grey dot while items are left to do (`todo_dot` in Settings › UI, from the app feed's `todos`). A disc opaque over the icon, since the corner of Changes' icon is under it. The TUI's green dot on its gear, while a browser is attached or you are away on a chat app, is left out: the window has no gear that holds those, and the Settings icon opens one sheet.
- **Look** (`design/widgets.md`): the rail and the list are two floating cards (`card`), 8 px apart and in from the window's edges, each a layer of its own as the panes and the dock are (asked for on 2026-10-26; until then they shared one card, as macOS 26 floats a sidebar with the traffic lights in its corner). Both start below the title row, level with the panes: the traffic lights span 16 to 76 px on macOS 26, wider than the rail's 44 px card, and putting them in the list's card would move them out of the window's corner, past the rail. So they and the title strip (the mark and the wordmark) sit on the ground above the cards. Linux has no traffic lights (`design/window.md`), so there the title strip reaches back over the rail's column to the window's edge and the mark takes their corner, centred over the rail's icons, with the wordmark 8 px after it as on a Mac (`mark_lead`, 2026-11-04); leaving their 76 px empty looked like a missing control, and the mark alone over the rail with the wordmark over the list split the lockup by 22 px. The strip, with the tab row, is the title bar that moves the window there. A cobalt wash lights the top of each card. The rail's first icon is level with the "Projects" header and its last with the footer's buttons, so the two cards read as a pair. In a window too short for the rail, its card runs past the window's bottom rather than letting the icons spill out of it; the list's card shrinks and its rows scroll. The selected workspace and agent are a rounded cobalt tint, as macOS fills a selected sidebar row; the glowing bar and gradient they had went with the site's square shapes. Only the deepest shown row is tinted: when the shown workspace lists its tabs (or its shown agent), the tab is lit and the workspace only takes the brighter, medium-weight name, so two cobalt blocks do not stack (asked for on 2026-10-29, with "everything is too big" and "the nesting does not show"). Children hang from a thin guide line under their parent's chevron (`rows::nested`), workspaces under a project in the tree and tabs or agents under a workspace, since an indent alone did not read as nesting. The lists are 12.5 px with a 1.4 line height (gpui's default, 1.618, made every row about 30 px tall), project names medium rather than semibold, a tab is one line whose details are left out when they repeat its title (a `zsh` tab running zsh), and a workspace counts its tabs only while folded or when its tabs are not listed under it. Names not selected are in the second grey, details in the third. The section title is "Projects" in title case, and the footer's buttons carry icons, "Agent" in cobalt as the main action.
- **`SideState` is shared with the tab bar and the panes**: a menu, a rename and a right press on a pane (a menu on release, or a move onto another pane) belong to the window, not to one row, so they live on `Root`.
- **`Target` names a row of the sidebar or of the tab bar**, so menus, renames and drags work the same way for tabs; its `flag` is how cornercase's commands name it (`--group`, `--project`, `--workspace`, `--tab`).
- **`cmd` is `store::args`** under the name the window's files already used; it was a copy of it.
- **Row buttons appear only on hover, just before the row's figures** (`rows::trailing`, the row's end: a workspace's pull request tags, `⇄N`, `↓N` and status, a project's count and status, a group's count and chevron, a folded agent's `+N`), or at the row's end when it has none, so they take no room from the name the rest of the time and never cover what the row says. `slot` counts leftwards from there. They are opaque rounded chips (`raised`), centred on the line the figures sit on (a workspace's first line): over a translucent row, the name would show through a translucent button. While a row is hovered its name makes room for them and ends in its ellipsis a gap before them instead of running under them; the figures never move, so the pull request tag stays under a press even if the room goes during it (gpui stops reporting a pressed row as hovered once the pointer moves). At rest an end without figures gives its gap back to the name, so a row looks as it did before the pointer came. Until 2026-11-01 the buttons sat at the row's right end, over the figures: hovering a workspace hid its pull request tag (asked to fix on that day), and other rows their count, status, chevron or `+N`.
  - **The room follows `on_hover`, the chips a paint-time `group_hover`**: gpui applies hover styles only when painting (`design/widgets.md`), so a margin set under hover would not move anything. `SideState::hovered` keeps the row under the pointer; leaving a row clears it only while it is still the hovered one (`after_hover`), since one mouse move can report the next row first. It follows the pointer's moves, so after a scroll with the pointer still, the row now under it shows its buttons over the end of its name, and the one hovered before keeps its room, until the pointer moves.
  - Rejected: reserving the buttons' room on every row at all times, as the TUI does (`room` in its `ui/rows.rs`), which costs every name 26 px (50 px for a project's two buttons) for buttons shown on one row at a time; sliding the figures left to make room, since what the eye reads would jump on every row the pointer crosses and a tag could move from under a press; hiding the figures under the buttons while hovered, as Zed's list items swap their end, which hides the pull request tag and its click.
- **New workspace (`dialogs.rs`) has the Run on row** once a machine is listed (`design/machines.md`): it asks the machine nothing, says where the workspace lands there, and sends `new-workspace NAME --machine NAME --project ID [--worktree]`. It stays open with a busy button until the machine answers, where the local one closes at once and leaves the job to the status bar, so a refusal leaves what was typed.
- **A project's `+` offers New workspace… and Start a flow…** (as the TUI's `+ New` of the workspaces, #263 upstream), on the project row's hover `+` and on the Workspaces header's `+` of the stacked and side-by-side arrangements; the project's menu starts with both. A flow makes a workspace, so it sits beside the new workspace, not in the Projects header's `+`, which opens projects and groups. The menu opens on the press, as the row's `⋯` and the Projects header's `+` do, and Start a flow… opens the start dialog on that project (`design/flows.md`, checked live there on 2026-11-01).
- **A fold shows at once** (`sidebar/folds.rs`, a stopgap until #549 upstream): the view's lines reach the window about 500 ms after any command, since the command's own connection opens the server's pacing window (measured on 0.12.225: the command answers in 5 to 14 ms, the line follows at 501 to 517 ms, every time, idle or not). So a click on a chevron or a Collapse/Expand item flips the flag in the window's copy of the session, and each session that arrives gets it again until one agrees or the command has answered; from then on the server's word stands, so a fold changed elsewhere in the meantime is not overridden for good. A failed command flips it back and toasts. Rejected: flipping it with no pending list, since a session sent before the command arrived would unfold the row for half a second. Other actions read back from the session (rename, drag and drop) still wait; once #549 lands this goes.
- **A row's click focuses it, a double click renames it in place**, a right click or its `⋯` opens its menu. A group's click folds it instead.
- **Renaming**: enter saves, escape or a click elsewhere cancels. A group needs a name, so an empty one is not sent; a project, a workspace or a tab goes back to its default name (folder, branch, program) when emptied, as the placeholders say.
- **Drag and drop** (`cornercase move`): a row dropped on a sibling takes its place, so it lands after the target when it came from above (as the TUI's drag does) and before it otherwise. The end of a list is `--last`, or for a project `--into GROUP` (or `--ungroup` outside any group). Workspaces stay in their project and tabs in their workspace. A project dropped on a group goes into it; a group dropped on a project moves next to that project's group. The empty space below the rows takes a project out of its group, or a group to the end.
- **Order**: projects without a group first, then each group with its projects. Groups without projects are listed too, so projects can be dragged into them; a project whose group no longer exists counts as loose.
- **No filter of its own** (removed on 2026-10-31, when the user asked how it differed from ⌘F and whether to drop it). Until then a field above the projects (⌘P) kept the rows holding every word of the query. Search's Names (`design/search.md`) finds the same rows by the same details and more (groups, a shell tab's programs), ranks them, picks any of them with ↑ ↓ where the filter's ↩ went only to the first workspace shown, and lists the whole session when empty; the field took a row of the sidebar at all times for a second way to do one job, and the TUI has none. What went with it: keeping the list narrowed while working on its rows, and words matched in any order across a row's details. ⌘P shows the pull request since 2026-11-01 (`design/terminal.md`).
- **A project row's status is only one that needs you** (`Project::attention`: waiting, failed, done, pending), in the tree when folded and in every other arrangement, as the TUI's project rows: a working moon there would be lit whenever any agent of the project runs, which says nothing a glance at its workspaces does not (asked for on 2026-10-30). Workspace and tab rows still show working.
- **Workspace rows** show the branch only when it differs from the name, the changes, the tab count, the agent count when folded, and how many agents were closed (reopened from the menu). The pull request's tags (`#485✗`, a conflict's `#486!`, a stack's `2/3`) open its dialog, and hovering them gives the title, the summary and the failed checks (`design/pull-requests.md`). `⇄N` says it changes files another workspace changes too: hovering it lists them, and a click, like **Merge check…** in the row's menu, opens the merge check (`design/shared-files.md`).
- **A tab's and an agent's details name the account after the model**, once there is an account (`design/accounts.md`, The account of a running pane). `compacted` goes where the percentage goes, and a tab's row ends with the memory of its panes before how many they are (`design/terminal.md`, Tabs); an agent's row does not, as in the TUI, since the figure is what a tab costs. A shell tab named after its program leaves the program out, so with memory on it reads `zsh  2 MB`, not `zsh  zsh · 2 MB`. A machine's tab rows read the same fields through the same function (`design/machines.md`), so they say all this too, and this window's `model` and `context` switches apply to them; whether a pane there carries a figure is that machine's `memory` setting. Those tab rows were not checked live with a figure (a unit test covers them).
- **Agent rows** are a tree of who started whom inside the workspace. The title is the conversation's, without the spinner or mark the agent's program puts in front, falling back to the tab's name; a folded agent shows how many it hides.
- **The sidebar keeps its own tooltip** (`Tip`, wider and in 12 px) rather than `notify::tip`, whose look differs; they could become one widget in `src/ui/`.
- **The Projects `+` says what importing a VS Code workspace waits on** (#673 upstream, `design/upstream-gaps.md`): the TUI's picker imports a `.code-workspace` as a group, `open` takes folders, and the system's folder picker can carry no line, so the note sits under the menu's entries (`VS_CODE_NOTE`).
- **A worktree's menu says what Run setup waits on** (#672 upstream), where the TUI has **run setup**. It shows on every worktree except one running its setup, since `view` does not say whether a project has a setup, and reading `config --json` before a menu opens would only hide a faint line.
- **Opening a project into a group** runs `open PATH --focus --json` to learn the new project's id, then moves it into the group and unfolds the group. Without a group, a plain `open PATH --focus` runs through the view. On another machine (`design/remote.md`) the folder picker would show this Mac's disk, so the path is asked for instead (`dialogs::ask_path`), and the menus drop Open in….
- **Open in…** on a project's or a workspace's folder, and in the pane menu on the folder its program is in (`path_items`, cornercase 0.12.158): the apps of `design/files-panel.md`'s Opening elsewhere that open a folder, so Finder ("file manager" on Linux, "File Explorer" on Windows, `desktop::show_label`), then VS Code, Cursor, Zed and Sublime Text when they are installed, without the default app; one app alone is its own entry, not a submenu of one. Each runs as a job whose failure is a toast. On another machine only Copy path stays. Checked live on 2026-11-06 (debug build, cornercase 0.13.1, Linux x86_64 under Xvfb with lavapipe, a private server, a logging `code` first on the `PATH`; no macOS, no Windows): a project, and a pane after `cd src`, which opened that folder.
- **Dialogs ask cornercase what would be lost before acting**: removing a worktree reads `close --workspace ID --remove-worktree --check --json` (uncommitted changes, a lock and whether its process still runs, a flow's role worktrees, the teardown) and stays disabled until the changes (`--force`) and the lock (`--unlock`) are acknowledged; closing an agent reads `close --agent --pane ID --check --json` to list what stops and what keeps running elsewhere, and says nothing on disk goes. Deleting an empty group is a plain confirmation; one holding projects offers to keep them (ungrouped) or close them too.
- **A machine's rows** (`sidebar/machine_rows.rs`, `sidebar/machine_acts.rs`; what they act on is in `design/machines.md`): a machine's workspace sits under the project it pairs with, after its own, with its tabs under it and a tag at its right: the machine's icon, drawn with the Machines sheet's Lucide icons, and its name, in the muted grey, the row's end kept to half the row so a long name is cut rather than the workspace's. A machine's project with no pair is a project row at the end of the list, after every group and outside them, with the same tag, its count and its mark.
  - **The two-column arrangements** (decided 2026-11-06): the Projects list counts a machine's workspaces in the project they pair with and adds the projects alone at its end; the Workspaces list shows the shown project's, ours first, then theirs. While a machine's place is picked, that list follows the place and not the view (`Listing::shown`): one under a pair shows the pair's rows and lights the pair's project, which is how ` Go ` from under another project lands on a row you can see; one in a project alone lists that project's and lights its row, and the header has no `+`, since nothing can start there yet. Nothing here moves meanwhile, so clicking anything of ours goes back. Upstream moves `active` to the pair for the same column (`follow_pairing`); here that would move the view, and the local selection stays as it was so that going back is one click and finds it unchanged. In the tree they sit in the same places, nested, with no fold state shared with ours.
  - **A machine's tabs are listed whatever `tabs` says**: with tabs on top a workspace of ours lists its agents and the bar holds its tabs, and the bar holds a machine's only while one of its tabs shows (`design/machines.md`), so the sidebar is the place to pick one from anywhere else. Their look is ours (`tab_title`, `tab_details`, `badge`), one line with the model and context, without the account mark, which reads this server's accounts. No agents section: it stays this server's, as a click there focuses a pane here.
  - **Not linked**: every row of that machine is at 0.55, the opacity of a worktree being removed, and its spinner is the still icon (`ui::still_badge`), since nothing says the agent is at work; a tab with an agent says `<status> · seen 2 min ago` on a line of its own instead of its details, which `Root::keep_seen_counting` redraws each second while such a machine is listed. Local rows are never dimmed.
  - **Buttons**: only a machine's workspace has them, on hover, `✕` and `⋯` just before the tag (`End`, `hover_button`, as ours); a right click opens the same menu, a double click renames. A machine's tabs and a project alone have none and open no menu. The `✕` is on the row rather than in a menu, as upstream's, because the menu holds only **Rename workspace**: every other item of ours acts on this computer.
  - **Folds live here** (`ThereFolds`, `SideState`): in memory, keyed by the machine's name, the folder and the workspace's name, so one survives the machine's restart and not this window, and two workspaces in one folder (`main` and `notes` on a plain checkout) fold apart, where upstream's key by path folds them together. A project alone folds too, in the tree. They are not sent anywhere, as nothing asks a machine to fold from here, and start open, ignoring the machine's own `collapsed`.
  - **No drag**: a machine's rows are not `draggable` and have no `drop_target`, so a row of ours over them shows no landing line and a drop does nothing, and they never join `siblings`, whose ids are ours. Upstream's drop there lands after our last row; refusing is simpler and nothing is lost, as the row of ours above them is a target.
  - **Side by side really is 42 %** since this change: its `card()` is `flex_1`, whose basis of 0 beat the width, so the projects' column had been as narrow as its content (102 px on the bench) and a tag left `w…`. It now sets its basis, as the stacked top list does.
- **A worktree being removed stays, dimmed** (`Store::removing`), with `removing…` and a spinner where its detail goes, and takes no click, menu or drag until the session drops it, as the TUI's row does. The dialog closes on confirm and the removal runs beside the queue (`Store::remove_worktree`). Start agent here, Reopen closed agents, Close project, Close workspace, Close its agents (its check too), Close agent, deleting a group with its projects, Forget closed agents, closing a tab or a pane that runs an agent, creating a group (its Create reads `Creating…`) and opening a project are jobs the status bar names while they run (`design/connection.md`).
- **Icon and colour** come from `group style ID --json`, which lists the choices; a click applies at once and updates the shown choice without waiting for the session. A new group takes the next icon and colour, changed afterwards from its menu.
- **A workspace's agents close together and come back later** (#430 upstream, `forms/agents.rs`): the menu's **Close its agents…** first runs `agents close --check --json` and asks with what it would close; when that cuts a turn, starts an agent over (no conversation to resume) or stops other panes of the same tabs, it names them and the button says **Close anyway**, which passes `--force`, as the TUI asks only in those cases. **Reopen N closed agents** stays, and **Forget N closed agents…** asks before `agents forget --yes`. Checked on 2026-10-29 with cornercase 0.12.225 on the `--check` answer only; closing was not run on a real agent.
- **It follows cornercase's own UI settings** (`sidebar/look.rs`): `sidebar`, `tabs` and `agents_section`, read from `config --json` when the window connects and again whenever the session's `config.revision` grows, so changing them in Settings (this app's or the TUI's) changes the window at once. Rejected: a setting of this app's own, since the TUI and the window would then disagree on one person's layout, and the setting's note already says how projects, workspaces and tabs are laid out. A failed read keeps the last look and toasts. Until the first answer the window draws the tree with tabs on top and no agents section, as it did before it read them.
- **The rest of Settings › UI** (read the same way, asked for on 2026-11-06, an audit against the TUI): `model` and `context` take their parts out of the details of tabs and agents, in the tab bar too, and `compacted` follows `context`, as in the TUI (`Look::model_of`, `Look::share_of`); `counts` takes the number off a project's row, a machine's project's too, and a folded group's; `todo_dot` the rail's dot; `dim_inactive_panes` the fade of inactive panes, and of their chips (`design/terminal.md`, Drawing). `account_mark` and `account_switcher` were followed already (`design/accounts.md`). All on until the first answer, as their defaults are, so nothing blinks off at start. `memory` needs no reading: `view` carries the figure only while it is on. **Left out**: `icons` (`auto`, `on`, `off`): it says which windows may draw Nerd Font glyphs, a fact of the terminal a TUI's window runs in; this window draws its icons from its own SVGs (`design/widgets.md`), so nothing can show as a box and `off`'s "plain symbols" has nothing to fall back from. `working_icon` (`moon`, `spinner`): the TUI's two glyphs, `◐` still or `⠋` turning, for a column of text; the window's working icon is its own orbit, asked for twice (`design/widgets.md`), and following it would still that orbit for everyone, since `moon` is the default and a default cannot be told from a choice. `icon_bar`: the rail is always there.
- **The four arrangements** (`sidebar`): `tree` is one list (groups, projects, workspaces, then tabs or agents); `projects_on_top` and `workspaces_on_top` stack, in one card, the list of projects and the shown project's workspaces, each half of the room and scrolling on its own; `side_by_side` puts them in two cards, the projects' about 42 % of the width (140 to 260 px). Side by side keeps a width of its own (456 px until dragged), so switching to it does not squeeze the workspaces into the one-column width. Outside the tree, a project row has no fold: it shows its workspace count and its status, a click shows it, and the shown one is lit, as the TUI's projects column. The line between the stacked lists drags (`ui::split`, `Edge::Bottom`, the size of the top list; half each until dragged, a double click goes back to halves); its grip is centred on the drawn line, not on the top of the lower list, since the line sits 6 px into it. The top list's size is its flex basis, not its height: it is a `flex_1` column, and taffy takes the basis over the height.
- **Tabs** (`tabs`): `sidebar` lists every tab under its workspace (status, the agent's conversation or the tab's name, then the agent, model and context, or the program, and how many panes) with a `+ Tab` row, and the bar above the panes keeps only the split buttons; a tab row renames, drags, opens its menu and focuses like a tab of the bar. `top` keeps the bar and lists under each workspace only its agents, as a tree of who started whom.
- **Worktrees** are workspaces here as in the TUI: every arrangement draws them as workspace rows marked `⎇`, with their branch, pull request, changes and how far behind they are, and the Worktrees sheet lists every worktree on disk (`design/worktrees.md`); no setting changes how they are shown.
- **Agents section** (`agents_section`): every agent of every project at the bottom of the list's card, 200 px high until its line is dragged (`Edge::Top`, measured from the bottom so the section keeps its height when the window grows; a double click goes back to 200) and scrolling on its own, in sidebar order as one tree (`Session::agent_rows`, the same walk as a workspace's), so a child started in another worktree hangs under its parent. A row names its `project › workspace` (the workspace only when the project has more than one) unless its parent is in the same workspace, then the agent, its role, model and context; it has the agent's menu (with View context, `design/context-dialog.md`), its fold and a `✕` on hover that opens Close agent. No agents shows "No agents running." rather than hiding the section, so the list does not jump when the first one starts.
