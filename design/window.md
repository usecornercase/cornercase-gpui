# Window

Code: `app.rs`

- **One connection, two streams**: `cornercase start-server --theme -` finds the running server or starts one and gives the terminals our colours (so programs pick a look that matches what we draw); only then does `view` open, and `surface --view` follows the view's id. A lost connection retries every 1.5 s (another machine backs off, `design/remote.md`); each new `view` bumps `generation`, so lines from an old stream are dropped.
- **Where the first view starts**: `--tab`, `--workspace` or `--project` on our own command line, else on what cornercase's window shows. Later views (after a reconnect) start on what the server's view settles on.
- **Another server replaces `Root`** (`Root::switch`, `design/remote.md`): the dialog asks through `Store::connect`, and the window builds a new `Root` with `replace_root` rather than resetting every field, keeping only the sidebar's and dock's widths and the zoom. The status bar (`app/status.rs`) names the server and opens that dialog.
- **A surface that ends is opened again** after 400 ms: the view lives on (it settles on a neighbour when its tab closes), so the stream is reopened for the same view instead of opening a new view. When it ends with the same error as last time (a server too old for `surface`, say), it waits 5 s instead, so a lasting failure does not run `cornercase` twice a second. The error stays in the terminal's place, in full, until a frame arrives: a toast alone left "Waiting for the terminal…" there with no reason given. It is toasted once, when it changes.
- **A view that shows no tab streams nothing** (`app/surface.rs`): cornercase pauses the surface while the view shows a project or workspace with nothing open, and refuses to open one then. So the surface opens only once the session shows a tab, and one that ends while the view shows none waits for a tab without a toast or a retry (`Feed::Waiting`); the terminal's place says no tab is open (`design/terminal.md`).
- **A window in the background does not count as seeing its tab**: `view-set --view ID --hidden` on deactivation and `--visible` on activation, so agents in the shown tab notify while the window is behind others. Since #461 upstream this works with `--view`, so it is always sent, with no fallback for a server that refuses it.
- **Keys go back to the terminal** when a dialog or sheet closes: `Root` watches `Store` and refocuses its own handle on that transition, since gpui would otherwise leave the focus on the closed view.
- **Panels are built once** and kept in `Root::panels`, so a closed panel keeps its scroll, filters and fields when it opens again.
- **Floating surfaces on one ground** (`design/widgets.md`): `Root` paints the ground, and the rail's and the sidebar's cards, the panes, the dock and the tab pills float on it, 8 px (`GAP`) apart and in from the window's edges. The title row is 44 px (`TITLEBAR_HEIGHT`) of ground, the traffic lights moved to 16, 16 so they sit centred in it on a Mac (on Linux the mark takes their corner, below), and every card starts below it (`design/sidebar.md`). The status bar is quiet text on the ground under the panes, without a band or a line.
- **The sidebar and the dock take the width they are dragged to** (`design/widgets.md`), and a drag always leaves the terminal room for a few dozen columns. Changes, Files and TODO share one width, so switching between them does not resize the terminal and the programs in it; the Browser and the Flow panel each keep a width of their own, half the window up to 720 px until dragged, since a page and a flow's diagram need the room (`design/browser.md`, `design/flows.md`). The dock's width includes its right margin, so its handle sits on its card's left edge and a drag still measures from the window's edge (428 px, a 420 px card, until dragged). The sidebar no longer shrinks with the window, so its handle stays on its border; the row holding it and the workspace area is `min_w_0`, or its content's width (a long path in the status bar) would push the dock out of the window.
- **The bell** in the shown tab is a short flash of the terminal's edge, not a sound: the sound is for agents' notices (`design/notifications.md`).
- **Drags**: `Drag::Program` sends presses, motion and releases to a program that reads the mouse; `Drag::Select` is cornercase's own selection, in cells of the pane; `Drag::Divider` moves a divider of the tab's split.
- **`CORNERCASE_GPUI_OPEN=<panel>`** opens a panel at start, to check it by eye; `CORNERCASE_GPUI_FONT_SIZE` sets the terminal's font size (13 by default); `CORNERCASE_GPUI_OPACITY` the opacity of the terminal's background (0.65 by default, kept between 0.3 and 1; 1 makes it opaque, and below the ground's 0.55 it shows the ground alone, `design/widgets.md`), read once at start, as Ghostty's `background-opacity` is. Translucency depends on what is behind the window, so it is a setting rather than one value for everyone. On Linux, where the ground is opaque, it is how strongly Tokyo Night covers the ground, with no floor (`design/widgets.md`).

## Linux

- **The window draws its own frame** (`app/frame.rs`, 2026-11-04). It asks for client-side decorations (`WindowDecorations::Client`), as Zed does, and draws a title bar's work whenever gpui says the frame is the app's (`Decorations::Client`). GNOME on Wayland, the most common desktop, has no server-side decorations at all, and gpui 0.2.2 cannot tell a window that asked for them whether the compositor will draw them: without `xdg-decoration`, `request_decorations(Server)` still reports `Decorations::Server`, so a window that asked for server-side ones opens on GNOME with nothing to move, resize or close it by. Asking for client-side ones is safe everywhere because gpui falls back by itself where they cannot work: on X11 without a compositor, or under a window manager that does not support `_GTK_FRAME_EXTENTS` (openbox), it keeps the window manager's frame and reports `Decorations::Server`, and a Wayland compositor with `xdg-decoration` (KDE, sway) may answer server-side; the app then draws none of its own. Rejected: server-side with a client-side fallback (gpui does not say when the fallback is needed) and a setting to choose (Zed has one; nothing asks for it yet).
- **The frame** is a 12 px margin around the window (`set_client_inset`) that holds a soft shadow and the resize grips, outside what shows as the window, as GNOME's own windows have; 20 px corners, the cards' 12 plus the 8 px between them, so the curves stay parallel; and a 1 px `border` line. A side that is tiled loses its margin, the corners beside it their curve, and a tiled or maximised window its shadow (`Tiling`). The grips are eight invisible strips with the resize cursors, each edge reaching 4 px into the window and each corner 16 px further, that start `start_window_resize`. A sheet's or dialog's veil takes the window's corners, or it would darken the margin's corners into squares, and the window clips what it holds, so the rail of a window too short for it ends at the window's edge rather than in the margin. The window opens larger by its margins (`frame::outer`), so it shows 1280 × 800 as on a Mac: at 776 px the rail did not fit.
- **The title row is the title bar** under a frame of the app's own: its empty ground (the sidebar's title strip, which reaches over the rail there, and the tab row between its tabs and its buttons) moves the window when dragged, zooms it on a double press, and opens the compositor's window menu on a right press where there is one (`window_controls().window_menu`). The move starts on the first motion after the press, as Zed's does, since a move started on the press takes the pointer, so the release that ends a click, and the second press of a double press, could be lost. A press that a tab, a button or the window's buttons took first claims the row (`Grip`, a gpui global) rather than stopping the event, so a tab can still be dragged and every press still gives the keys back to the terminal, as on a Mac.
- **The window's buttons** (minimise, maximise or restore, close) sit at the right end of the tab row, after the split buttons: 24 px circles with a faint fill, lighter on hover, and icons of the app's own (`assets/icons/window-*.svg`) drawn to the size of Lucide's ×. Only what the compositor allows shows (`window_controls()`, from Wayland's `wm_capabilities`); close always shows. The set and its order are fixed: GNOME's `button-layout` (close alone by default) is not read, as it is one desktop's setting and a window that only closes surprised more than three buttons did.
- **The window's background** is `Transparent` on Linux, for the frame's margin and corners (Wayland makes a window with client-side decorations transparent anyway; X11 under a compositor needs it), with the ground painted opaque inside them (`design/widgets.md`).
- **`app_id` is `cornercase-gpui`** (Wayland's `app_id`, X11's `WM_CLASS`), the name a `.desktop` file matches the window with.
- **Checked live** on 2026-11-04 in Debian trixie (Docker, Xvfb, lavapipe), gpui 0.2.2. X11 under openbox (no compositor, no `_GTK_FRAME_EXTENTS`): openbox's frame and none of ours, `WM_CLASS` `cornercase-gpui`. X11 with no window manager: no frame at all, since nothing can draw one. X11 under xfwm4 with its compositor (as GNOME on Xorg): our frame, its margin and corners see-through over another window, a drag on the title row moves the window, a double press maximises and restores it, the restore button, resizing from the right edge and the bottom-right corner, minimise, close, xfwm4's window menu on a right press, and a press dragged off a split button leaves the window where it was. Wayland under weston (no `xdg-decoration`, as GNOME): the frame, the resize cursors on the edges and corners, moving, maximising, restoring, resizing, minimising, closing, and a sheet's veil keeping the corners. X11 at 2× (`Xft.dpi: 192`): the frame and the layout scale with it.
- **Not checked**: GNOME, KDE and sway themselves (Mutter's and KWin's frames, KDE answering server-side, sway's tiling), fractional scales on Wayland, a real GPU. Known limits: a menu snapped to the window's edge can reach 4 px into the margin, since gpui snaps it 8 px from the surface's edge; a window shorter than the rail shows a few pixels of it past the bottom-left curve, as the window clips to a rectangle.
