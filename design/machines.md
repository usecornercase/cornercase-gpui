# Machines

Code: `src/model/machines.rs`, `src/panels/machines.rs`, `src/panels/machines/`, `Store::machines` and `Store::take_machines` (`src/store.rs`), `Counting` (`src/ui/counting.rs`). A machine's workspaces in this window have a section of their own below.

- **What the sheet covers** (⇧⌘N, the rail's server icon): the other computers this server keeps a link to (`cornercase machines`, Settings › Machines in the TUI), each with the state of its link, and adding, editing, removing, retrying and updating them. A machine's workspaces in the sidebar, a machine's tab and `run on` in the dialogs are below; Changes and Files read a machine's workspace (`design/changes-panel.md`, `design/files-panel.md`); usage per machine is in the Usage sheet (`design/usage.md`).
- **Two different things, kept apart in words** (upstream's "Two lists of machines", its `design/remote-windows.md`): this sheet is the server's list, the machines it links to while the window stays where it is; ⇧⌘K (`design/remote.md`) moves the whole window to another server, keeps no list and has its own words ("Connect to a server", "This Mac"). A machine added in one is not offered in the other, as in the TUI. Each one says so: the sheet ends with "To move this whole window to another server instead, press ⇧⌘K", and the dialog with "This moves the whole window there. To keep it here and link another computer instead, use Machines (⇧⌘N)". Both can be in use at once: with the window on `devbox`, the sheet lists the machines `devbox` links to.
- **One list, in `Store`** (`Store::machines`, `model::machines`): `status --app --follow` carries `machines` in every line, and `follow_app` parses them there, once; a key the server leaves out when the list is empty reads as no machines. Whatever else needs the machines (the sidebar, the dialogs) reads this field and calls `Machine`'s methods for their words; nothing parses the feed again. The panel never polls. Every change's `--json` answer goes through `Store::take_machines` too, so a row shows at once what was done rather than when the next line of the feed comes; a feed line that crossed it is corrected by the next one.
- **`here` comes from `machines --json`, read when the sheet opens**: the feed has no such field. Until it answers, the rows show without the version beside `this computer`. A failed read keeps the feed's rows and says why above them; with neither there is a "Try again".
- **Where the code is**: `model/machines.rs` the list, a machine's icon and its words, for anyone; `machines.rs` the sheet's state, loading, the changes, the top of the page and the empty page; `machines/rows.rs` the card and its rows; `machines/form_view.rs` the form; `machines/form.rs` the form's checks and texts and `machines/data.rs` the commands, questions and row notes, both tested without a window. The cards, rows and form pieces are the Accounts sheet's (`panels/accounts/widgets.rs`, opened to the crate for this), so the two sheets read alike.
- **A row** is the icon, the name over `destination · folder`, the state in words beside its dot, and the actions in fixed places. **The words are the TUI's**, in `Machine::words`: `connecting…`, `linked · 0.13.1`, `unreachable · seen 2 min ago` or `· not seen yet`, `older · 0.12.200`, `updating…`. Dots: green linked, orange older, red unreachable or a problem; `connecting…` and `updating…` take a spinner instead, since they are running. The TUI's grey for unreachable is a mark among monochrome text; here red is what the other sheets use for what failed, as Accounts' signed out. What the list says of why goes under the row: `error` (what ssh said, `cornercase was not found on vps`, the same server as another machine, a failed update), in an error line, always shown, where the TUI shows it only for the selected row; and `Its server runs 0.13.0 until it restarts there`, which the TUI adds to the state, as a hint: it would not fit the state's column.
- **`seen` counts up by itself**: `unreachable · seen 2 min ago` is worked out from `seen` when drawn. Nothing is asked for it. The clock is this computer's and `seen` the server's: a server ahead reads as `0 s`.
- **A link that fell says when it tries next** (`retry_at`, `NextTry`, the same in `machines --json`, the feed and a `view` line's `machines`): `reconnecting in 4 s` counted down from a time the server keeps still until that try fails, so the feed sends a line when it moves and never one a second. Where it shows: the machine's card, a second line under the state in the sheet (the state's column has no room for both, and the row keeps `seen` where it was), a tab's note in the sidebar, which says `waiting · reconnecting in 4 s · seen 2 min ago` in that order, since a narrow sidebar cuts the end, and the strip at the top of Changes and Files while they read one of its workspaces (`vps unreachable · reconnecting in 4 s`, the view line's `retry_at` as the card reads it, carried in `MachineRead`). Three meanings, as upstream's: **a time ahead** counts; **a time reached or passed** means the try is under way, so it says `reconnecting…` with a spinner and never `0 s` or a negative (upstream keeps the time for that reason, rather than dropping it when the wait ends); **no time** on a machine not linked means a person has to act there, so nothing is promised: `older` says so in its state, and `error` says why under the row and on the card. The strip is the exception: with no time it still says `reconnecting…` with a spinner, as before it counted, since `Reach::Down` takes `unreachable` and `connecting` alike and the strip does not read the list's `error`. The strip's other wait, the panel's own `Retry` after a command fails while the link says linked, does not count: it says `did not answer · reconnecting…`. That is a known limit, kept out of #73's scope, which names the link's next try. It differs from the card, which counts its own stream's back-off (`Standing::RetryIn`: the time is ours there, so it can be said), and from upstream's `design/remote-windows/machine-panels.md`, "Retries", which counts this case too. `Machine::words` stays the state alone, as the TUI's: a Run on chip has no room for the count. Whole seconds are enough: `retry_at` has milliseconds, but the redraw has a phase of its own, so the count can lead by under a second and the try can read as under way that much early. A window on another server reads a time from that server's clock against this computer's, as `seen`.
- **Only what shows ticks** (`Counting`, `ui/counting.rs`): a draw that put a counting text on screen says so (`Counting::drawn`), and at the end of the view's `render` one redraw is asked a second later if any did, while a draw with none drops the one waiting (`Counting::keep`). So no timer runs while every link is up, nor while the texts are out of sight: a closed panel, a fold, another sheet, a machine down whose tabs have no agent, a card past its time, a window not drawing. `Root` keeps one (`Root::keep_counting`) for the card (`card_counts`), a tab's note (`MachineSeen::tab_note_counts`) and, with the sheet or a dialog open, a listed machine (`Machine::counts`), read from `Store`; Changes and Files keep one each for their strip (`on_machine::strip`), which counts while its note shows the link's time. Rejected: the two loops this replaces, which woke each second for as long as the window lived and looked for something to redraw; a timer turned on and off where the state changes (a line of the feed, a line of `view`, the tab falling, a fold, a sheet), since any of them missed leaves a frozen count or a timer running, where every one of them already redraws. The sheet has no timer of its own, as a parent's draw draws its views (gpui's `AnyView`, not cached here). **A docked panel keeps its own** (#73): whether its strip counts depends on the panel (Changes' scope), which `Store` does not hold, and a panel draws after `Root::render` has returned (gpui draws an `AnyView` while laying its parent out), so the window's `Counting` would hear of it a frame late. Rejected for that: one `Counting` in `Store`, which would also put a draw's state in what every part shares; and Files' poll each second, which redraws only when an answer changed something, where Changes has none. With the card and a strip counting at once the window can redraw twice a second, once for each.
- **`problem`** is an entry `config.json` got wrong: `problem · no link` in red, and under it `In config.json: …` with cornercase's reason. It has no link, so no Retry (cornercase refuses it) and no Update; Edit, which shows at once what is wrong, and Remove stay.
- **Rows are keyed by their place, not their name**, as the TUI's: a hand-edited file can hold two entries with one name, and the second must show its own problem. Every command finds a machine by its name and takes the first (`position` in cornercase's `app/control/machines.rs`), so Edit or Remove on the second would change the first, a machine the person did not pick: that row offers neither and says it can only be fixed in the file (`Listed::shadowed`).
- **Retry** (`machines retry NAME`) shows while a machine is `unreachable` or `older` and has no problem: never when linked, and not on `connecting…`, which is already trying and would make the button flash on every new machine. cornercase answers at once and the state may not change at all (a host that refuses again says the same thing, and only the time moves), so the button says `Retrying…` for 2 s after the answer: otherwise a press looks like nothing happened. The state changes when the link says so. Rejected: a toast (for each press, for something the row is already about).
- **Update…** (`machines update NAME`) asks first, since it installs software on another computer and cannot be undone from here, with what it does: `cornercase update` there, nothing restarts. It runs as a job, beside the queue, which the status bar names: it can take minutes, and it waits for the install. A row shows `updating…` both while its own job runs and when the list says `updating`, so an update started from the TUI or a script reads the same; that row's Edit and Remove rest meanwhile. **Its failure is a toast**, as every row action's; the list's `error` then holds the same reason for as long as it matters, under the row. A failure kept in the sheet as well would say the same thing twice, in words that differ only by the destination (cornercase hides it in the command's answer, not in the list), and would not show with the sheet closed, where an update that takes minutes often ends. Only that row waits: the others stay live, unlike Accounts' one change at a time, since an update can outlast everything else a person does there.
- **Remove** (`machines remove NAME`) asks first, through `dialogs::confirm`, saying that the server there keeps running and nothing there is touched, though the TUI does not ask: AGENTS.md's rule, and a machine's name, destination and folder are lost from the list. It comes back only if added again.
- **The form** opens inline, under its row for Edit and at the card's end for Add (also when the machine being edited went away meanwhile, so the form does not vanish; its save then says `no machine is called…`). `Add machine` sits in the top bar: the sheet has one list, so a card header would only repeat the sheet's title. It has the name, the icon, the ssh destination and the folder for clones, each with its rule under it and its refusal in that place. Enter saves from any field, Tab moves between them and Escape cancels (`design/terminal.md`). The sheet scrolls to the form as it opens, only as far as there is more to show (`ScrollAnchor`): at the default 800 px its buttons were cut off from the fourth machine on, and with more machines its first field too, where the keys go. **The fields belong to the open form and go with it**, rather than staying on the panel as Accounts' do: a hidden field that keeps the focus keeps the keys from `Root`, and a second Escape would not close the sheet. Gone with the form, gpui clears the focus and `Root` takes it back (`keep_the_keys`, `design/window.md`).
  - **Values are checked here, so the form says why at once** (`form::refusals`, with cornercase's words: `machines::valid_*`, `name_taken`, `destination_taken`): a wrong value as it is typed, an empty one only when the form is sent. They are checked again by cornercase, whose refusal shows above the buttons. Its long table of invisible characters is left to it; the form only checks spaces and control characters, what can be typed. Each value is trimmed, as cornercase does. Edit sends only what changed (an icon `config.json` holds and this window does not know stays until it is changed), and a form with no change closes without a command.
  - **The icon is drawn with this app's Lucide icons, in a row of segments with their names**, not the TUI's glyphs: `☁ ▭ ▣ ◫ ⌂ ◆` come from whichever font each system falls back to, at another size and weight, and macOS can draw `☁` as a colour emoji; the row of pictures reads at a glance where `▣` and `◫` need their names; the rest of this window's chrome is Lucide, and a mask icon takes a state's colour. Each stays the TUI's picture where one exists: `cloud` for server, `laptop`, `monitor` for desktop, `house` for home server, `diamond` for other. `◫` mini has no such picture; a small flat box, `hard-drive`, is the nearest, shared with the rail's Worktrees. The JSON holds the glyph and the commands take the name (`MachineIcon`), and an unknown glyph reads as the first, as in the TUI. The rail's icon is `server`, a rack, for the sheet rather than for one machine.
  - **Hosts are offered from `~/.ssh/config`** (`dialogs::ssh_hosts`, the reader ⇧⌘K uses, without patterns and git forges), as chips under the destination that fill it: the first 8 that hold what is typed, without those another machine uses, since a destination must be unique. With the window on another server they are not offered: that server's ssh makes the link, and this computer's config says nothing about it. The rule then says `What ssh takes on devbox`, and the note `connect once by hand from devbox`.
  - **The form says once what the link needs**: Linux or macOS, cornercase of this server's version or newer, and connecting by hand once with `ssh you@host true`, naming the destination as it is typed. The version is `here`'s rather than the 0.13.0 machines came in, since a machine older than this server links as `older`; until `here` answers, it is the oldest cornercase this window runs on (`cli::MINIMUM`).
- **The empty page** says what a machine is for and what it needs, in one sentence each, with `Add machine`, in the look of the empty Queue (`ui::emblem`). Pressing it shows the card, with `this computer` and the form.
- **On another server** (`cornercase-gpui --remote devbox`, or after ⇧⌘K) the first row is that server's name rather than `this computer`, which would name the wrong machine, and says it is the server this window is connected to; the commands go there as every command does (`design/remote.md`).
- **What does not work yet is said where a person looks for it**: a machine must run Linux or macOS (the link needs `sh` there; a Windows computer can link to machines but cannot be one), in the form's note and on the empty page. A Windows machine's link fails as any wrong destination does, with what ssh or cmd printed under its row.
- **Settings has no way here**: Settings does not point to Accounts either, and `config --json` lists no Machines page (upstream: its rows are entries of a list, not settings).

## A machine's workspaces in this window

Code: `src/model/machines/seen.rs` (the `machines` of a `view` line, `ThereId`, `Aim`, `MachinePlace`, what a command on a machine says), `src/store/machines_there.rs` (reaching a machine, acting on it, the picked place, its home), `src/sidebar/machine_rows.rs`, `src/sidebar/machine_acts.rs`, `Whose` in `src/panels/worktrees/row_removal.rs`, `src/terminal/machine_card.rs`, `Notice::there` in `src/notify/feed.rs`. How the rows sit in each arrangement is in `design/sidebar.md`; the parsing in `design/session-model.md`; the notices in `design/notifications.md`.

- **A machine's ids are another server's, and the types say so** (`ThereId`): the same numbers mean other things here (on the bench workspace 7 is `main` on both, and `paired` is 8 beside a project 8), and sending one to this server would act on the wrong workspace without an error. `ThereId` has no `Display`, no conversion to or from `u64`, and `key()`, the only text anyone outside the module can get, is `there-7`, which no command takes. `Aim` (machine, boot, id) is the one place that writes such an id into a command (`rename_args`, `close_args`), and `store::machines_there` the one place that runs those. `Tab` and `PaneInfo` take their id's type (`Tab<Id = u64>`), so a machine's tabs read and draw through the functions ours use (`loudest`, `agent_pane`, `tab_title`, `tab_details`, `agent_details`) with nothing copied, and code that wants a tab of ours still says `Tab`. Rejected: a machine's workspace as a `Workspace` with a `machine` field, as every `session.workspace(id)` would then have to tell them apart (upstream's `Hierarchy` keeps them out for the same reason); `Workspace` and `Project` generic too, since they hold ids in fields a machine's rows never draw (`shared`, a pull request's stack); plain mirrored `ThereTab` and `TherePane`, which would copy the row's words.
- **Running a command on a machine** is `cornercase --remote DESTINATION …` with the machine's `destination` (`design/remote.md`): `cli::run_on` and `cli::stream_on` take a `Target`, and `store::run_on`, `store::run_job_on` (a job) and `store::follow_on` sit beside `store::run` and friends. `cli::stream` and `store::follow_until` are those with the window's own target, so nothing is unused until the machine's tab streams.
- **`machine_target` says whether a machine can be reached from here**, a plain function tested without a window: a window that is itself on another server (`--remote`, ⇧⌘K) gets `vps is linked from devbox: its address means nothing from this computer`, since the list and its destinations belong to that server and its ssh; a machine not linked gets `vps unreachable`, `vps runs an older cornercase` or `vps is connecting`; one gone from the list says so. `Store::reach_machine` adds that the workspace is still in the snapshot, of the same `boot`, and that no action on it is waiting. Every way in asks it: the menu shows its answer as a line at rest above **Rename workspace**, also at rest (`Entry::Resting`, which the keys skip, at an item's size since it stands for one, where `Entry::Note` is a smaller remark beside items that work), and the `×` and a save of the form make it a toast with `: nothing was sent`. Nothing is queued for when the machine comes back. The rows show the same from another server; only acting is refused.
- **`boot` goes with every close and rename** (`--boot`, #677 upstream, in `rename_args` and `close_args`), as upstream's window sends the `boot` of the snapshot with the action: the machine compares as it acts, nothing is done on a stale one, and the command exits with 4. That closes what comparing here left open, the moment between the last line and the command, in which a command that spends a whole ssh session getting there could close or rename whatever took the id after a restart. The dialog and the form keep an `Aim` with the `boot` they were opened on, and sending still compares it with the last line first, which says so without a session and also covers a workspace gone. **Both refusals read alike**, `vps restarted: nothing was sent` (`stale_note`, and `cli::stale_boot`, which tells status 4 from any other failure): what happened and what to do are the same, whichever noticed first. Rejected: showing what the machine prints, which names two boots and tells a script to read `status` again. A dialog stays open when its workspace leaves, rather than closing as upstream's do; its button then answers with that toast.
- **A worktree's removal goes by its folder and sends no `boot`** (#74). `boot` guards ids, which a restart renumbers; `worktrees remove` names a worktree by its folder, which no restart renumbers (#671, #677 upstream). The machine matches the folder against its own list and checks every risk again as it removes, so what became risky after the question is refused unless `--force` or `--unlock` was sent after the person saw why, and `--delete-branch` deletes only the branch still checked out. The check runs when the dialog opens, so the question shows what the machine sees then. `Store::reach_machine` still runs before the check and again before the removal. What changes: no status 4, and a restart the window has not heard of removes the folder the person saw. A machine this window can reach is at 0.13.19 or newer, so `worktrees remove` (0.13.18) is always there. Rejected: keeping `close --remove-worktree --boot`, which cannot delete the branch, so the machine's question would differ from ours with no safety gained. Rejected: asking upstream for `--boot` on `worktrees remove`, since it has no id to guard.
- **Rename** is the rows' inline form (`Renaming::There`), starting from the name the machine gave it (`custom_name`, else `name`), from the `⋯`, a right click or a double click; `rename --workspace ID -- NAME`, the `--` so that a name starting with `-` stays a name. Empty goes back to the branch's name, as for ours. It shows when the next line brings it, in about a second on the bench; while it waits, the menu rests and `×` says it is still waiting.
- **`×` always asks**, also where a workspace of ours only has a menu item, since what closes there is not seen here and cannot be undone. A plain workspace gets `dialogs::confirm` naming the machine (`Close the workspace notes on vps?`), what stops, and the closed agents the snapshot counts. A worktree gets the question ours gets, from `worktrees remove --check --json --path FOLDER` there, so what it names is what that machine sees, with `on vps` after the name, and Also delete the branch. A check that fails, or finds nothing that can go, closes the dialog with a toast, as ours does.
- **While a close or a removal waits** (`Busy::leaving`), the row is dimmed, says `removing…` with a spinner, and takes no click, menu or button, as a worktree of ours being removed. **A success keeps it so until the snapshot drops it** (`Jobs::left_there`, `keep_leaving_there`), so it never comes back for a moment, and says `Closed notes on vps`, since what happened is not seen here; a removal's toast is the Worktrees sheet's, with `on vps` (`Removed shop › feat/rows on vps and the branch feat/rows`). A close that fails brings the row back with `vps: …` in a toast; a removal that fails or is refused says `Could not remove … on vps: …` or `Did not remove … on vps: …`. A long removal has no cap of its own here: ssh's keep-alive ends one whose link dies (`design/remote.md`), and the job says it runs meanwhile; upstream's 10 min cap needs its own connection.
- **The click picks a place, and the store keeps it** (`Store::machine_place`, `MachinePlace`: `machine`, `boot`, `workspace`, `tab`, and the workspace's path and name to find it again). A workspace's row picks its active tab, a tab's row that tab, a project alone its first workspace. It is the only selection a machine has: the view's `shown` does not move and nothing is sent to this server. Each line follows it (`MachinePlace::followed`): by id within a `boot`, a closed tab falling back to the active one; by path and name after the server there restarted, since its ids start again; dropped when neither finds it, and the window is simply back on its local tab.
- **What comes after the click**: `Store::pick_machine_place` and `Store::leave_machine_place` are the only ways in and out, and `Root::machine_place_changed` (from `store_changed`, in `app/machine.rs`) the one place told of a change, where the machine's streams open and close; a machine's tab, below, is what it starts.
- **Going back**: anything this window does that moves its view drops the place. `cli::moves_view` (`focus`, `go`, or `--focus` among the words) is asked by the queue (`Queue::send`, which tells `Root` through `Msg::ViewMoved`) and by `store::run_busy` and `Store::run_aside`, the ways such a command runs beside it. So a click on the row of what already shows, which changes no line, goes back too, and so do ⌘1, a local notice's ` Go `, a Search result and `new-tab --focus`. Rejected: comparing `shown` between lines, since the lines trail a command by about 500 ms (`design/sidebar.md`, folds), so a local click followed at once by a machine's row would bounce back when its line came, and a local tab closing by itself would throw the card away.
- **The local tab rests behind the place**: the terminal area reads the machine's connection while one is picked, so no key, paste, drop, click or wheel reaches a pane here (`design/window.md`, `design/terminal.md`), and ⇧⌘W closes the machine's tab, never this one; a plugin's popup opened from elsewhere waits, and one run from this window goes back to the local tab (`design/plugins.md`). The view is told `view-set --hidden`, as for a window behind others, so an agent in that tab that needs you or finishes gets its notice, whose ` Go ` comes back to it (upstream: `visible_tab`); a bell there is a toast, not a flash over the machine's tab. The window title, the agents section and the docked panels other than Changes and Files stay on what is here; Changes and Files read the machine (`design/remote.md`).
- **The card** shows while there is no live frame to draw: `runs on vps`, what the active pane's agent is doing (`claude is waiting for you`, `claude is working`, `claude failed`; else the first agent; `no agent there`), and the folder with the machine's home as `~`. While the machine is not linked: `vps unreachable` and `claude keeps working there` (`its shells keep running there`). What it says of the connection is below.
- **The machine's home** is not in the `machines` of a line, so it is read once per `boot` from `status --json` there when a place is picked (`Store::ask_machine_home`), through the same `machine_target`. It only shortens a path, so a failure is not a toast: the full path shows, and the next pick asks again.
- **A machine's agents fold there** (#663 upstream, `PaneAim`, `sighted_pane`, `Store::fold_on_machine`; the rows are in `design/sidebar.md`): the arrow and the menu send `collapse` or `expand --pane ID` through `--remote`, the pane's own `collapsed` flag on the machine, so this window, the machine's window and its scripts fold one team alike. Its workspaces fold here in memory (`ThereFolds`, `design/sidebar.md`), since that fold says how this sidebar lays out a machine's rows among ours and the machine's window lays out its own; an agent's fold is one team's, whichever window looks at it.

## A machine's tab in this window

Code: `src/app/machine.rs` (`MachineTab`, what happens on each stream end, keys, steering the view), `src/app/connection.rs` (`Connection`), `src/terminal/machine_card.rs` (the card), `machine_tab` and friends in `src/tabs.rs`, `Queue::on` and `Queue::nowhere` in `src/cli.rs`, `src/terminal/machine_menu.rs` (the pane menu), `Root::close_agent_there`, `Root::view_context_there` and `Root::publish_tab` in `src/app/machine.rs` (the menu's agent entries and what the shown tab tells `Store`), `src/store/machine_read.rs` (`TabThere`, `AgentReach`, `reach_agent`), `src/sidebar/forms/checked.rs` (the close-agent check, here or there: `AgentCloseThere`), `src/dialogs/context.rs` (`ContextThere`), `src/dialogs/closing.rs` (the words of closing). How the two connections sit in the window is in `design/window.md`; what a key and the mouse do in `design/terminal.md`.

- **The tab as it is there, through a second connection of its own**: upstream's answer for a client like this one (its `design/remote-windows/machine-tab.md`): "the local server does not pass the machine's `surface` or `input` on". So `cornercase --remote DESTINATION view --cols C --rows R --tab ID` stays running, and its first line gives the view's id and the machine's session; `surface --view ID --patches` streams on a connection of its own (a view with a size claims its tab there, a `surface --tab` never sizes anything); `Queue::on` is an ordered queue for that target, with its typist and `requests` process. `events --host --json` is a fifth, below. Each is one `cornercase --remote` and one ssh, or none extra under the user's `ControlMaster` (`design/remote.md`).
- **What belongs to a connection is one struct** (`Connection`: its queue, view, surface, grid and size): `Root::own` is the window's, `MachineTab::conn` the machine's, and `Root::shown` what the terminal area reads, so `terminal/` asks `self.shown()` for the grid its pane ids come from and the queue they go to, and the same code draws and types on both. A frame's ids are its server's, and the only way from one to a queue is through that one connection. Rejected: swapping fields in and out of `Root` (a hidden copy of the state of each), and `if machine` beside every `self.queue` (a missed one sends a machine's pane id to the pane with the same number here, with no error). `Root` has no `queue` or `grid` any more, so the compiler found every place that had to choose.
- **Another tab, or another workspace of the same run, moves the same view** (`focus --tab ID --view VIEW`, or `--workspace` when it has no tab; `Root::steer_machine`), which the surface follows; another machine or another `boot` opens a new connection (`plan`). Upstream, and the first design here, opened a new view for another workspace; a focus on the view costs one line on an open link rather than four ssh sessions, and the machine's server already allows it. The sidebar's rows, the tab bar and its keys all pick a place in the store, and only `machine_place_changed` sends anything.
- **The tab bar shows the machine's tabs** while one shows, with no `+` and no `×` (`tabs.rs`), from the local server's snapshot (`Tab<ThereId>`), the same data the rows read. ⌘1…9 and ⇧⌘[ ⇧⌘] follow that bar. A workspace with no tab streams nothing and the card says `no tab is open there`.
- **Keys typed while another tab arrives wait for its first frame** (`Arriving`), and then go to its active pane: on a link of 100 ms the frame shown is still the tab left, and its pane is what those keys would reach, an agent's prompt perhaps. At most 1,024 wait, for at most 20 s, and they are dropped with the toast when the tab is asked for again, the stream falls or its `focus` is refused: keys landing late in another tab are worse than keys lost (upstream's reasoning). The mouse and the wheel send nothing meanwhile, and a press held on the tab left is released before the `focus` goes.
- **Losing it** (`machine_fell`, `follow_link`, `link_step`, plain and tested): any stream ending, or the machine's `state` leaving `linked` in a `view` line here, stops every stream of the connection and points its queue at `Queue::nowhere`, so nothing typed can be sent or kept, whatever else the code does. The last frame stays, dimmed to 40 % and with no cursor, under the card. `vps unreachable: nothing was sent` is the toast for what is typed, and the toast for a tab still opening says so.
  - **A stream that fell with the link up** comes back through `cli::backoff` (1, 2, 4, 8, then every 15 s; a frame starts it over), opening all of it again: if its ssh fell the others are about to, and a view without its surface is a tab that cannot be drawn. The card says `reconnecting in 4 s`, counted down each second, with the last thing that stream wrote on stderr under it. Upstream says `reconnecting…`; the time is ours here, so it can be said.
  - **A link that fell** comes back when a line says `linked`, at once, and the card says what `retry_at` says (`Standing::LinkDown`, above): `reconnecting in 4 s`, then `reconnecting…` with a spinner while that try runs, and the time moves when it fails. With no time a person has to act there, so the card promises nothing and says the list's `error`, as upstream's; an `older` machine's title says it already. `connecting` has no time either and is running.
  - **Our streams may outlive the link**: the local server's link and our connections are different ssh sessions, and on the bench a hold file refuses new ones while the open ones stay up. The tab still goes down with the link, as asked: the snapshot is what the rows and the card read, and a tab live under a row that says `unreachable` would say two things at once.
- **After the machine's server restarted** the place follows the workspace by path (`MachinePlace::followed`, `design/machines.md` above), and its new `boot` opens a new connection, with no last frame: those shells are gone. A `view` line or a frame of another `boot` than the place's is never shown (a retry can reach the new server before the local snapshot says it restarted, and an old `--tab ID` can name another tab there): it counts as a fall and tries again. Nothing restarts on either side from here; the link is what starts the server there (`machines --help`). While a machine is not linked nothing says a workspace is gone, so a place stays through a local server's restart, when the first line lists the machine with nothing yet.
- **Closed there**: the next snapshot no longer lists the workspace, so the place drops and the window is back on its local tab with `main is no longer on vps` (unless this window closed it, whose own toast has said so). A closed tab settles the view on a neighbour there and the place on the workspace's active tab; a `focus` follows if they differ.
- **What counts as seen there**: the machine's view is `--visible` while this window is active and no file's full screen covers the tab, and `--hidden` otherwise (`tell_machine_visible`, and `view --hidden` for one opened meanwhile), so an agent there notifies as an agent here does behind other windows.
- **Sizing**: `Fit` sizes whatever connection shows (`Connection::size`, its own: the local view keeps its size while hidden, and is told the new one when it shows again). `view-set --cols --rows` goes on the machine's queue, coalesced, and a try opens at the size wanted by then. A split there changes how many cells fit (`design/terminal.md`), so a tab with splits gets two sizes as it opens, as a tab here does when it is switched to.
- **The local surface stops, the local view stays active and hidden**: `view-set --inactive` is what its help offers, and it was rejected: it gives the local tab back to whoever claimed it before, else the window, so every visit to a machine would resize the programs here twice and repaint an agent's screen. A view that stays active keeps its claim, and its `surface` opens again in one local process, its first answer a whole frame. Keeping the surface running was rejected too: a frame that nothing draws is parsed and stored, at up to 60 a second while an agent works, and each one would redraw the window. Coming back shows the last frame for the few milliseconds that takes, or the note when the view moved meanwhile.
- **Theme: the machine gets this window's colours while one of its tabs shows (and again when the theme changes meanwhile), and they are cleared when leaving** (`theme -` on its queue at each new view, `cli::leave` as `Root::switch` does, from `Root::take_back_colours`, also on quit). Programs there ask the server for the colours they are drawn with and pick their look from the answer, and this window draws their cells: the reason `start-server --theme -` exists holds for any server this window draws. Without it, a machine whose first window was a light terminal tells vim to be light, and this dark window draws it. The price, accepted: while shown, the machine's own TUI hands out our colours to programs that ask, and `--clear` goes back to its first window's rather than to another GUI's (upstream's rule, one colour set per server). A link down at leaving, or a window that crashes, leaves them until the next `--clear` or the server stops, which is harmless: they are colours. A leaving costs one session, one-shot, as for a server left; a tab opened and left within a round trip may clear before it gives.
- **Reading a program's own copy**: a program that reads the mouse selects by itself and copies with OSC 52, which the machine's server takes for its own host, so `events --host --json` (the fifth stream, on the queue's fence) brings it here: a pane of the frame shown copies to this computer's clipboard, cleaned, with `claude on vps copied to the clipboard`. Its bell flashes the tab as ours does. A stream that fails is left alone: a tab that falls for the clipboard would be worse than a copy that does not come. Upstream does the same (`machines/stream/copies.rs`).

## Starting work on a machine

Code: `src/dialogs/run_on.rs` (`RunOn`, the row), `src/dialogs/run_on/clone.rs` (the Clone form), `src/model/machines/run_on.rs` (`Offer`, `There`, `Starting`, `Answer`, `Awaited`), `src/model/machines/run_on/checks.rs` (`Checks`, `Wanted`, `Hold`, `Trackers`, `AgentNote`), `src/model/machines/run_on/issue.rs` (`IssueThere`, `follow`), `src/model/agent_settings.rs` (`Agents`, `Modes`), `src/model/machines/run_on/clone.rs`, `src/model/machines/seen/spot.rs` (`Spot`, what a machine's project writes into a command), `src/store/run_on.rs` (the jobs, the selection, a dialog over a dialog). The dialogs' own parts are in `design/issues.md`, `design/flows.md` and `design/sidebar.md`. Upstream's decisions are in its `design/remote-windows/run-on.md`; this says what differs because this window has only the command line.

- **A row and not a setting** (`RunOn`, an entity each dialog holds, drawn only while `Store::machines` lists one or one is chosen): this computer, then every machine with its icon. Upstream's reasons hold: never saved, since a machine chosen by habit would start work somewhere nobody looked; a machine down stays chosen, dimmed, and refuses (`vps unreachable: nothing was sent`), rather than falling back to this computer, where a start pressed a moment later would run unasked; one that left the list refuses by name, with the row kept so that this computer can be picked. The choice is a name, since the command line has no link's id as upstream's window has: a machine listed again under that name counts as the same one. Rejected: a plain struct in each dialog, since the answers need an entity to land on, and three dialogs would each carry the same callbacks; the row emits `Chosen` and the flows' answer, and the dialogs read the rest.
- **Why a machine cannot be picked** is on its chip, in `Machine::words` (`down · unreachable · not seen yet`), with what ssh said in its tooltip (`Offer`). A machine linked with no snapshot yet says `not seen yet`, since nothing can be paired without one. From a window on another server (`--remote`, ⇧⌘K) no machine can be picked: asking goes through `--remote` from this computer, where its address means nothing, as for acting on its rows above.
- **A project with no `repo` cannot run elsewhere**, and the row says so under the chips: the identity is the only way to pair it with one there. `repo` and `remotes` are in the `view` line, read here on `Project`.
- **Pairing**: the first project there whose `paired` names this one or whose `repo` equals this one's (any case). `paired` alone was not enough: it names only the first project here of that repository, so a second clone here would read as not there, offer a clone, and the machine would refuse the folder.
- **Asking** runs `cornercase --remote DESTINATION …` as jobs the status bar names and the row says (`Asking vps for its accounts…`): `account list --check --json`, and for the machine's id of the project, `issue sources --json --project ID` and `flow list --json --project ID`, only what the dialog shows (`Wanted`), so New workspace asks nothing. `Checks` decides when, as plain data: on choosing a machine (again, too, which is how a person asks anew), once each; again when the machine restarts (another `boot`), when the project there changes (listed after a clone), and for what failed, when the link comes back or `Start` is pressed. A failure is not asked again at every line of the feed. An answer of an earlier choice or about another project is dropped. An issue's start also asks `config --json`, for the machine's modes, at once, as for its accounts. An issue's start and Start a flow from an issue ask `issue read KEY --source S --json --project ID`, for what its templates make of the issue, only once `issue sources` there says that tracker is `connected` or `failing`: one that lacks a token is already named, and the read would fail with the same reason. So `heard` asks again for what came due (a job ends before its answer arrives, so the store's change came too early).
- **The agent**: `agents` says whether it is installed there and `providers` whether the chosen account is `signed-out`. The note says `claude is not logged in on vps`, and never blocks `Start`, since the server there was started by ssh with a short `PATH` and the agent may be there in the tab's shell (upstream's reasoning). A set token variable (`tokens`) silences the signed-out note, since one wins over every account; upstream checks each agent's own variables, a table this window does not have. An unknown login, or an answer without `agents`, says nothing.
- **The login tab** is upstream's: a `new-tab` in the workspace at the project's root there, named `claude login`, with `--account claude=FOLDER`, closing the dialog and selecting the tab. It runs the agent's own `command` as `agents` gives it, quoted for sh (a machine runs Linux or macOS), since `account --help` says to log in "by running the agent in a tab of that account"; upstream's table of login subcommands (`claude auth login`) is its own. An agent that is not installed gets a plain shell, `Open a shell there`, to install it. Rejected: `account add --login`, which makes a new account rather than logging in the chosen one. With no workspace there nothing is sent and the toast says so.
- **The tracker**: a source not `connected` is named under the row (`vps has no GitLab token for shop`) and `Start` sends nothing, as the machine would refuse. **`failing` differs from upstream**: it says why but does not hold, because it is what the machine's last read left and nothing here can read it again, so the row would stay shut after the tracker came back; a start that really fails comes back as a toast with the same reason. A check that failed holds with its reason; one still running holds quietly while the row says it asks.
- **Accounts** are the machine's: `AccountPickers` (`ui/account_picker.rs`) takes the list, here `AccountSettings::offered` of what the machine answered, under a line naming whose they are. Choosing another machine starts the pickers over, since an account's name means nothing elsewhere; a machine that cannot list them shows none rather than this computer's. Start a flow holds its start until they come, as upstream's: a run sent before would lose the account picked. An issue's start does not, since an agent's own login works.
- **A project not there**: `Start` opens the Clone form over the dialog (`Store::show_dialog_over`): the address (`upstream`, else `origin`, else the first remote with a host and a path, as the identity is chosen; `status` already takes secrets out), editable, and `folder/name`, the name worked out as `clone` will (`clone::folder_name`), for the folder there is what the machine lists. Back or Escape return to the dialog with the machine still chosen and what was typed, its focus restored (`dialog_behind`, `refocus`, which `Root` applies since `close_overlay` and `close_dialog` have no window). Cloning runs in the store, so it survives the dialog: the row says `Cloning on vps…`, `Start` does nothing, and a toast says how it went. `start` becomes possible once the machine lists it: until `paired` comes the answer's own project id is remembered with its `boot` (`Cloned`), so a clone under another address, which pairs with nothing, can still be started from this dialog, and `Cloned: waiting for vps to list it…` shows for at most 30 s rather than offering a second clone that would find the folder taken.
- **Git-ignored files, copied only when ticked** (`FileList`, plain and tested): the form lists what `clone --list-ignored --from FOLDER --json` answers, as a job, for the project's folder here (`Target::Local`: the files are on this computer, it needs no server, and `--remote` changes nothing for it). The form's words are upstream's (`machines/clone_form.rs`), saying `address` where it says `URL`, as the field here does. **Every box starts unticked**, as upstream's, since these are often secrets. A file over 1 MiB shows its size and `too big to copy` with no box, ignored folders are never offered, past the 200th a muted line says `… and N more not offered (200 at most)`, and a tick that would pass 8 MiB in all is refused with `Over the 8 MiB a clone takes.` under the form. A list that fails says why in red and the clone can still go, without files.
  - **`Clone` sends the ticked files** as `--file=PATH … --from=FOLDER` with `--remote DESTINATION`: this computer's `cornercase` reads them and the machine writes them with their permissions, on the link's one way, so there is no `scp` here. `=` so that a name starting with `-` stays a name. **`Skip files`** clones without them, and shows only while there is a box to tick, where upstream's always shows: with nothing to tick it would be a second `Clone`. Pressing `Clone` before the list came sends none, as upstream's.
  - **What the clone could not take** leaves the clone done and exits 3 with `unwritten` in the answer (`cli::run_partial_on`, the exit 3 other commands use for a partial answer): the toast is an error's, since a person's `.env` did not arrive, and says `Cloned shop on vps · not copied: .env (it is already in the clone)`, three at most with `… and N more`, one with a blank path left out and a blank reason reading `vps gave no reason`. A file refused before anything is cloned (gone since the list, grown past the cap) is the clone's failure toast, with cornercase's words.
  - **Windows**: `--list-ignored` and `--file` refuse there. The form runs the same command, so the refusal shows in red, and the intro, the one thing `Os::CURRENT` picks, says that the files stay behind, as upstream's has both. Not checked on Windows.
- **Starting** is `start AGENT --issue … --machine NAME --project ID --placed`, `flow run NAME … --machine NAME --project ID`, `new-workspace NAME --machine NAME --project ID [--worktree]`, through this server (`Spot::starting_args`, the one place besides `Aim` that writes a machine's id), never `--focus`, which moves cornercase's own window, nor `--view`, whose id means nothing there. It runs in `Store::start_there`, so a dialog closed meanwhile still gets its toast and its selection. On success the dialog that started it closes if it is still the one shown, compared by entity (`shown_dialog`); on failure it stays with what was typed, and the toast says `vps: …` with what the machine or ssh said. Rejected: a line in the dialog as well as the toast, which would say it twice.
- **What started is selected once the `machines` of a line list it** (`Awaited`, in `Store`, looked at on every line, `select_there`, which a click on its row also ends in), by the answer's tab (the orchestrator's for a flow), else its workspace. It is dropped by a pick of a person or a move of the view, by another `boot`, and after 30 s. That wait is all that is left: an answer names a tab this server's next line has yet to list.
- **`start --placed` answers as soon as the tab is open**, with the same ids, even while its agent asks to trust the folder (0.13.14; nothing is guessed from the project's tabs any more). So the dialog closes, the live tab shows and the question can be answered there, and the job ends with the answer rather than when the agent has its prompt. What comes before the tab still comes before the answer: making the worktree and reading the issue, for which the button says it is starting, as upstream's dialog does. `flow run` and `new-workspace` answer at once by themselves.
- **A launch that fails after that answer says nothing here, as in upstream's window**: nothing waits for it there. The machine's server logs `the agent did not start` and puts a refusal in its own window's toast (`drive_launches` in its `app/launches.rs`); neither crosses the link or reaches a command's answer. The tab is what tells: it is on screen, showing what the shell printed. So the toast says `claude is starting on #7 on vps`, not `started`. `status` has no field for a launch (upstream's note on `--placed`).

- **The machine's modes and templates** (#77):

The red line in the Run on row says what could not be read. Neither read holds Start, since what is left alone is the machine's anyway.
  - **Rejected**: filling `issue_branch` here from `config --json`, which would reimplement the server; asking upstream for `prompt` in `config --json`, since its text would still need filling here; this computer's modes, prompt or branch on a machine, since what it showed was not what ran; `TextField::keep_saved` for the prefill, which changes Escape.
